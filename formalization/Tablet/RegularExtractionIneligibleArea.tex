\begin{lemma}[Area of ineligible partition rectangles]
Let $G$ be a finite simple graph and let $U,W$ be vertex sets of size $n$.
Let $\mathcal P$ be an equipartition of the full vertex set, which has size
$2n$, and put $m=|\mathcal P|$. Suppose $1\leq l\leq m\leq n$,
$\gamma,\alpha>0$, every part has size at most $3n/m$, and
$\mathcal P$ is $\alpha$-uniform: at most $\alpha m(m-1)$ ordered
distinct pairs of parts fail $\alpha$-uniformity.
Let $E$ consist exactly of pairs $(S,T)$ of parts satisfying $S\ne T$,
$\alpha$-uniformity, $\gamma|S|\leq|U\cap S|$, and
$\gamma|T|\leq|W\cap T|$. Then
\[
 \sum_{(S,T)\in(\mathcal P\times\mathcal P)\setminus E}
 |U\cap S||W\cap T|
 \leq (4\gamma+9\alpha+6/l)n^2.
\]
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Write $a_S=|U\cap S|$, $b_S=|W\cap S|$, and $c_S=|S|$.
The parts are disjoint and cover all vertices. Intersecting with $U$
or $W$ preserves disjointness and gives a cover of that set. Consequently
$\sum_S a_S=\sum_S b_S=n$ and $\sum_S c_S=2n$.
All three quantities are nonnegative, and $a_S,b_S\leq c_S$.

For a nonsubstantial $U$ chunk, $a_S<\gamma c_S$. Hence
$\sum_{S:a_S<\gamma c_S}a_S\leq\gamma\sum_S c_S=2\gamma n$.
Summing its rectangle areas over all $T$ multiplies this bound by
$\sum_T b_T=n$, giving $2\gamma n^2$. The identical argument with
$b_T<\gamma c_T$ gives another $2\gamma n^2$.

Let $N$ be the set of ordered distinct nonuniform pairs. Its cardinality
is at most $\alpha m(m-1)\leq\alpha m^2$. For each such pair,
$a_Sb_T\leq c_Sc_T\leq(3n/m)^2$. Since $m\geq l\geq1$,
division by $m$ is legitimate, and the total area over $N$ is at most
$\alpha m^2(3n/m)^2=9\alpha n^2$.

The diagonal area satisfies
\[
 \sum_S a_Sb_S\leq\sum_S c_S^2
 \leq (3n/m)\sum_S c_S=6n^2/m\leq6n^2/l.
\]
Finally, every pair outside $E$ is diagonal, has a nonsubstantial chunk
on at least one side, or is distinct and nonuniform. Pointwise its area
is at most the sum of the four corresponding nonnegative area indicators.
This does not require these causes to be disjoint: repetitions increase
the bound. Summing and using the four estimates proves the claim.
\end{proof}
