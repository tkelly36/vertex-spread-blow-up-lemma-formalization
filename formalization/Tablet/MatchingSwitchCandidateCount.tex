\begin{lemma}[Outgoing switching candidates]
Let $G$ be a graph on a finite vertex set, and let $U,W$ be disjoint sets of
the same positive size $n$. Suppose that $0<\varepsilon$, $4\varepsilon<d\leq1$,
and $G[U,W]$ is $(\varepsilon,d)$-super-regular. Let
$x_N:\{1,\ldots,k\}\to U$ and $y_N:\{1,\ldots,k\}\to W$ be injective,
with $k\leq dn/2$. Let $f:U\to W$ be a perfect matching satisfying
$f(x_N(i))=y_N(i)$ for every $i$, and let $x\in U$, $y\in W$ satisfy $f(x)=y$.
Write $\mathcal C_f$ for the ordered pairs $(a,b)\in U^2$ such that
$x,a,b$ are pairwise distinct, $xf(a),af(b),bf(x)$ are edges of $G$,
and $a,b\notin\operatorname{range}(x_N)$. Then, interpreting the finite
parameters $d,\varepsilon$ as real numbers,
\[
 (d-\varepsilon)(d/2-\varepsilon)^2 n^2\leq |\mathcal C_f|+3n.
\]
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Since $\varepsilon<d\leq1$, both parameters are finite; all inequalities
below use their real representatives.
By \noderef{PerfectMatching}, $f$ is a bijection. Let
$X_N=\operatorname{range}(x_N)$ and $Y_N=\operatorname{range}(y_N)$.
The indexing injections give $|X_N|=|Y_N|=k$.
Moreover, $f(a)\in Y_N$ if and only if $a\in X_N$: the reverse implication
follows from the prescribed equalities, while if $f(a)=y_N(i)=f(x_N(i))$,
injectivity of $f$ gives $a=x_N(i)$.
Define
\[
 A_0=\{a\in U:xf(a)\in E(G)\},\quad
 B_0=\{b\in U:by\in E(G)\},\quad
 A=A_0\setminus X_N,\quad B=B_0\setminus X_N.
\]
Equivalently, $A$ is the inverse matching image of the uncovered neighbors
of $x$ in $W$. The degree clauses of \noderef{SuperRegularPair}, together
with the bijection from $A_0$ to the neighbors of $x$ in $W$, give
$|A_0|,|B_0|\geq(d-\varepsilon)n$. Removing at most $k$ vertices gives
\[
 |A|,|B|\geq(d-\varepsilon)n-k\geq(d/2-\varepsilon)n.
\]
Set $L=A\subseteq U$ and $R=f(B)\subseteq W$, as subsets of the ambient
vertex set. The matching image preserves cardinality, so
$|L|=|A|$ and $|R|=|B|$. Since $4\varepsilon<d$ and $n>0$, both sizes
strictly exceed $\varepsilon n$. The regularity clause in
\noderef{SuperRegularPair} therefore gives density at least $d-\varepsilon$
between $L$ and $R$. By the density definition in \noderef{PairDensity},
the number of these edges is at least
\[
 (d-\varepsilon)|A||B|
 \geq(d-\varepsilon)(d/2-\varepsilon)^2 n^2.
\]
The factors used in this multiplication are nonnegative, and the density
denominator is positive because both sets have positive size.

The map $(a,b)\mapsto(a,f(b))$ bijects
$T=\{(a,b)\in A\times B:af(b)\in E(G)\}$ with those ambient edges:
injectivity follows from injectivity of $f$, and surjectivity follows from
the definitions of $L$ and $R$. Exclude from $T$ pairs with $a=x$, with
$b=x$, or with $a=b$. Each exclusion lies in a set of exactly $n$ pairs,
namely $\{(x,b):b\in U\}$, $\{(a,x):a\in U\}$, or
$\{(a,a):a\in U\}$. Their union thus has size at most $3n$.
The remaining pairs are exactly $\mathcal C_f$: membership in $A$ gives
$xf(a)\in E(G)$ and $a\notin X_N$; membership in $B$ gives
$by=bf(x)\in E(G)$ and $b\notin X_N$; membership in $T$ supplies $af(b)$;
and the three exclusions impose the required distinctness. Conversely,
these conditions put every candidate in $T$ outside the three excluded sets.
It follows that $|T|\leq|\mathcal C_f|+3n$, proving the claim.
\end{proof}
