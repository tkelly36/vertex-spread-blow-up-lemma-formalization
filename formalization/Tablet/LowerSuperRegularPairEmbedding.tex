\begin{lemma}[Transport of a lower super-regular pair]
Let $e:V\to W$ be injective, let $G,H$ be simple graphs, and let
$L,R\subseteq V$ be finite. Suppose that, for $x\in L,y\in R$,
$H(e(x),e(y))$ holds if and only if $G(x,y)$ holds.
Then $(L,R)$ is lower $(\epsilon,d)$-super-regular in $G$ if and only if
$(e(L),e(R))$ is lower $(\epsilon,d)$-super-regular in $H$.
The parameters may be extended nonnegative reals.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Injectivity preserves the cardinality of every finite set. For any
$P\subseteq L,Q\subseteq R$, the map $(x,y)\mapsto(e(x),e(y))$
is a bijection from the ordered $G$-edges of $P\times Q$ to the
ordered $H$-edges of $e(P)\times e(Q)$. Consequently their densities
are equal by \noderef{PairDensity}, including empty sets.
For subsets $A\subseteq e(L),B\subseteq e(R)$, take
$P=\{x\in L:e(x)\in A\}$ and $Q=\{y\in R:e(y)\in B\}$.
Their images are exactly $A,B$, and their sizes are therefore the
same. The strict size thresholds in \noderef{LowerRegularPair}
are unchanged, so its density lower bound transfers.
For each $x\in L$, the map $e$ also bijects its neighbors in $R$
with the neighbors of $e(x)$ in $e(R)$. For each $y\in R$ it
bijects the neighbors in $L$ in the other orientation. Transfer both
degree lower bounds in \noderef{LowerSuperRegularPair}, using
$|e(L)|=|L|$ and $|e(R)|=|R|$. Together with the transferred
lower regularity these are exactly the required three clauses.
Conversely, apply the target's subset bounds to $e(P),e(Q)$ for
each source test $P,Q$, and its degree bounds to $e(x),e(y)$.
The same bijections and cardinality identities give all source clauses.
\end{proof}
