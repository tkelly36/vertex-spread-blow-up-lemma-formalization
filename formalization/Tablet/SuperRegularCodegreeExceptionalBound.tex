\begin{helper}[Ordered codegree exceptions in a super-regular pair]
Let $G$ be a simple graph, let $U,W$ be finite vertex sets, and let
$|U|=n>0$.  Let $\varepsilon,d\in[0,\infty]$, with subtraction interpreted
as truncated subtraction.  Suppose $2\varepsilon<d$ and $(U,W)$ is
$(\varepsilon,d)$-super-regular.  Then at most $2\varepsilon|W|^2$
ordered pairs $(w,w')\in W^2$ have common-neighborhood size in $U$ outside
\[
 [(d-2\varepsilon)^2n,(d+2\varepsilon)^2n].
\]
No restriction on $|W|$ is required.
\end{helper}
\begin{proof}
Put $k=|W|$.  If $W$ is empty, the assertion is immediate.  Otherwise fix
$w\in W$ and put $A=N_G(w)\cap U$.  The degree clause of
\noderef{SuperRegularPair} gives
\[
 (d-\varepsilon)n\leq |A|\leq(d+\varepsilon)n.
\]
The inequality $2\varepsilon<d$ implies that $\varepsilon$ is finite and
$\varepsilon<d-\varepsilon$.  As $n>0$, it follows that
$|A|>\varepsilon n=\varepsilon|U|$, so $A$ is nonempty.
The displayed lower degree bound also forces $d$ to be finite: otherwise
$(d-\varepsilon)n=\infty>|A|$.  Thus all quantities in the following
density comparisons are finite, and all denominators used are positive.
Monotonicity and the degree bounds give
\[
 (d-2\varepsilon)^2n\leq(d-\varepsilon)^2n
 \leq(d-\varepsilon)|A|,
 \qquad
 (d+\varepsilon)|A|\leq(d+\varepsilon)^2n
 \leq(d+2\varepsilon)^2n.
\]
Define
\[
 L=\{w'\in W:|A\cap N_G(w')|<(d-2\varepsilon)^2n\},\qquad
 H=\{w'\in W:(d+2\varepsilon)^2n<|A\cap N_G(w')|\}.
\]
Suppose $|L|>\varepsilon k$.  Both $A\subseteq U$ and $L\subseteq W$
then exceed their respective regularity thresholds, so the regularity
clause of \noderef{SuperRegularPair} gives $d_G(A,L)\geq d-\varepsilon$.
Counting edges by their second endpoint, using
\noderef{FilteredProductRowCount} (and interchanging the two finite sums),
gives
\[
 e_G(A,L)=\sum_{w'\in L}|A\cap N_G(w')|
 <\sum_{w'\in L}(d-\varepsilon)|A|
 =(d-\varepsilon)|A||L|.
\]
Indeed, every summand satisfies the strict inequality by the definition
of $L$ and the first comparison above; also $L$ is nonempty since
$|L|>\varepsilon k\geq0$.  Dividing by $|A||L|>0$, as in the density
definition \noderef{PairDensity}, contradicts the regularity lower bound.
Consequently $|L|\leq\varepsilon k$.

Similarly, if $|H|>\varepsilon k$, regularity gives
$d_G(A,H)\leq d+\varepsilon$, while the second comparison and the
defining strict inequalities of $H$ give
\[
 e_G(A,H)=\sum_{w'\in H}|A\cap N_G(w')|
 >\sum_{w'\in H}(d+\varepsilon)|A|
 =(d+\varepsilon)|A||H|.
\]
Here $H$ is nonempty, so the summed inequality is strict.  Dividing by
$|A||H|>0$ contradicts the regularity upper bound.  Hence
$|H|\leq\varepsilon k$ as well.

For this fixed $w$, the bad second coordinates are exactly $L\cup H$,
because $A\cap N_G(w')=N_G(w)\cap N_G(w')\cap U$.
Their number is at most $|L|+|H|\leq2\varepsilon k$.
Finally \noderef{FilteredProductRowCount} expresses the number of bad
ordered pairs as the sum of these row counts over $w\in W$.
There are $k$ rows, giving at most $k(2\varepsilon k)=2\varepsilon k^2$.
\end{proof}
