\begin{lemma}[Outgoing switches have distinct resulting matchings]
Fix a bijection $f:A\to B$ and $x\in A$. On the set of ordered pairs
$(a,b)$ for which $x,a,b$ are pairwise distinct, the map
\[
(a,b)\longmapsto S(f;x,a,b)
\]
of \noderef{MatchingThreeEdgeSwitch} is injective.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Suppose the pairs $(a,b)$ and $(a',b')$ give the same function $g$.
By \noderef{MatchingThreeEdgeSwitchProperties}, evaluating at $x$ gives
$f(a)=g(x)=f(a')$, so injectivity of $f$ gives $a=a'$.
The same node gives $g(b)=f(x)=g(b')$ and says that $g$ is bijective.
Injectivity of $g$ therefore gives $b=b'$. In particular, $a$ is
recovered as the unique preimage under $f$ of $g(x)$, and $b$ as the
unique preimage under $g$ of $f(x)$. When $f(x)=y$, this is the unique
preimage of $y$. Thus the resulting functions, not just their cycles,
distinguish all outgoing candidates.
\end{proof}
