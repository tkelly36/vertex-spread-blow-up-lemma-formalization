\begin{lemma}[Independent unequal Bernoulli law]
Let $I$ be a finite index type and let $q_i\in[0,1]$ be nonnegative real
numbers. On $\Omega=\{0,1\}^I$ put the product of the Bernoulli laws with
success probabilities $q_i$. This is a probability measure; the coordinate
indicators $X_i$ are independent, $\mathbb E X_i=q_i$, and every centered
indicator $X_i-q_i$ has subgaussian moment-generating-function parameter
$1/4$, that is, for every real $s$,
\[
 \mathbb E\exp(s(X_i-q_i))\leq\exp(s^2/8).
\]
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Each Bernoulli law is a probability measure, including $q_i=0$ and $q_i=1$,
so its finite product is a probability measure. Evaluation at coordinate
$i$ preserves measure to that coordinate's Bernoulli law. Consequently
the integral of its indicator is the Bernoulli expectation $q_i$.
The coordinate functions are measurable, and functions of distinct
coordinates in a product measure are independent. Each indicator takes
values in $[0,1]$. Mathlib's proved Hoeffding lemma
\texttt{hasSubgaussianMGF\_of\_mem\_Icc} therefore gives the centered
indicator parameter $(1-0)^2/4=1/4$. These are exactly the claimed
independence, expectation, and moment-generating-function conclusions.
\end{proof}
