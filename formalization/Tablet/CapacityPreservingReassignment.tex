\begin{lemma}[Capacity-preserving reassignment of exceptions]
Let $V,J$ be finite types, $E\subseteq S\subseteq V$, and let
$f,b:V\to J$. Assume $|E|$ is strictly smaller than every old bin
$S_j=\{v\in S:f(v)=j\}$. There is a map $f':V\to J$, equal to
$f$ outside $S$ and equal to $b$ on $E$, whose bins in $S$ have the
same sizes as before, and each new bin has symmetric difference
of size at most $2|E|$ from its old bin.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Write $e=|E|$, $s_j=|S_j|$, $a_j=|E\cap S_j|$, and
$b_j=|\{v\in E:b(v)=j\}|$. The bins partition $S$, so
$\sum_j a_j=\sum_j b_j=e$; each of $a_j,b_j$ is at most $e<s_j$.
Remove all exceptional vertices from their old bins and put them
in their prescribed bins. Retain ordinary vertices provisionally.
Bin $j$ now has $s_j-a_j+b_j$ vertices, an excess $b_j-a_j$.
For each bin of positive excess choose exactly $b_j-a_j$ of its
ordinary vertices and remove them. This is possible because the
number of ordinary vertices is $s_j-a_j\geq b_j-a_j$.
The total number removed is
$D=\sum_{b_j>a_j}(b_j-a_j)\leq\sum_j b_j=e$.
As the signed excesses sum to zero, this is also the sum of deficits
of the negative-excess bins. Partition the removed set into pieces
of these deficit sizes and place each piece in its deficit bin.
Such a partition exists by listing this finite set and cutting the
list at the cumulative deficit sizes. All bins now have size $s_j$,
and no exceptional vertex was moved away from its prescribed bin.
Define $f'$ by this partition on $S$ and by $f$ elsewhere.

In a fixed bin, exceptional membership changes in at most
$a_j+b_j$ places. Ordinary membership changes in just one direction:
exactly $|b_j-a_j|$ ordinary vertices are removed or received.
Thus its symmetric difference has size at most
$a_j+b_j+|b_j-a_j|=2\max(a_j,b_j)\leq2e$.
If $J$ is empty, a map $V\to J$ forces $V$ empty and all conclusions
are vacuous. If $E$ is empty, the construction leaves every bin
unchanged. These cases require no division or positive size claim.
\end{proof}
