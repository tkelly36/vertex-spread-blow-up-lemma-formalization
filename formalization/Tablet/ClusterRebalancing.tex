\begin{lemma}[Cluster rebalancing]
For every integer $\Delta\geq1$ and $0<d\leq1$, there is $0<d'_0<d$ such that, for every $0<d'\leq d'_0$, there is $\varepsilon'_0>0$ such that, for every $0<\varepsilon'\leq\varepsilon'_0$, there is $\varepsilon_0>0$ such that, for every $0<\varepsilon\leq\varepsilon_0$, there is a finite $C_0$ with $1\leq C_0<\infty$ such that the following holds for $C\geq C_0$ (with $C/n\leq1$), every $r$, and every sufficiently large $n$ (depending also on $r$).

Let $(H,G,R,(X_i)_{i\in[r]},(V_i)_{i\in[r]},(A^i)_{i\in[r]})$ be a lower-$(\varepsilon,d)$-super-regular blow-up instance with lower-$(\varepsilon,d)$-super-regular candidacy graphs.  Suppose $\Delta(H),\Delta(R)\leq\Delta$, $|X_i|=|V_i|$, and
\[
 (1-\varepsilon)n\leq |V_i|\leq(1+\varepsilon)n
 \qquad(i\in[r]).
\]
Then there are a deterministic set $X\subseteq V(H)$ and a probability distribution on partial compatible embeddings $f:H[X]\hookrightarrow G$ whose restrictions $f|_X:X\to V(G)$ form a $(C/n)$-vertex-spread law (with all of $V(G)$ allowed), such that every $f$ in its support has the following property.  Thus, for any distinct $x_1,\ldots,x_s\in X$ and arbitrary $v_1,\ldots,v_s\in V(G)$, the probability that $f(x_j)=v_j$ for every $j$ is at most $(C/n)^s$.  On the literal residual vertex sets $V(H)\setminus X$ and $V(G)\setminus f(X)$, the induced graphs $H-X$ and $G-f(X)$, the unchanged reduced graph $R$, the exact residual fibers $X_i\setminus X$ and $V_i\setminus f(X)$, and the updated candidacy relation $A_f$ form a lower-$(\varepsilon',d')$-super-regular blow-up instance with lower-$(\varepsilon',d')$-super-regular candidacy graphs.  Moreover, for every $i$,
\[
 |X_i\setminus X|=|V_i\setminus f(X)|=\lfloor(1-\varepsilon)n\rfloor.
\]
Here ``exact'' means that the residual cluster maps are restrictions of the original maps and that a residual pair $(x,v)$ is allowed if and only if it belongs to \noderef{UpdatedCandidacy}.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Fix $\Delta,d$, and put $\widehat d=d/2$. This is positive and at most
$1$, so the principal statement of \noderef{PartialEmbedding} supplies
$b>0$ with $b<\widehat d$. Put $d'_0=\min\{d/4,b/2\}$.
Both entries are positive, and $d'_0\leq d/4\leq d/2<d$.
For $0<d'\leq d'_0$ we have both $d'\leq d/4$ and $d'\leq b$.
The same principal statement, at output reserve $d'$, supplies a finite
positive input-error cutoff $a_0$. Choose
$a=\min\{1/4,a_0/2\}$. Then $0<a\leq1/4$ and $a\leq a_0$.

Apply \noderef{RebalancingLossMargins} at $(\Delta,d,d',a)$.
It supplies $\varepsilon'_0>0$ bounded by $\min\{1/4,d'/2\}$ and,
for each admissible $\varepsilon'$, a positive $\varepsilon_0$.
Fix admissible $\varepsilon$, and let $n_a$ be its numerical order
threshold from that node. Independently apply the principal statement
of \noderef{PartialEmbedding} at input error $a$, not $\varepsilon$,
and output reserve $d'$. It supplies a finite $C_0\geq1$.
These choices are independent of $r$ and use no constants from the proof
of that theorem.

If $C=\infty$, choose $n_0=4$ for every $r$.
For $n\geq4$ the condition $C/n\leq1$ is impossible, since division
of $\infty$ by the finite scalar $n$ is $\infty$.
For finite $C\geq C_0$, let $n_p$ be the order threshold supplied by
\noderef{PartialEmbedding} at $(\Delta,\widehat d,d',a,C)$.
For every $r$ choose $n_0=\max\{n_a,n_p\}$; allowing an $r$-dependent
threshold does not require introducing $r$ into any earlier cutoff.

Fix $n\geq n_0$ and an instance satisfying the assumptions. Write
$N_i=|V_i|=|X_i|$. The numerical node supplies integers $m,k$ with
\[
 m\leq(1-\varepsilon)n<m+1,\qquad n/2\leq m\leq n,\qquad n\geq4,
 \qquad k\leq4\varepsilon n.
\]
In particular $m=\lfloor(1-\varepsilon)n\rfloor$.
Its window implications give, for every $i$,
\[
\begin{gathered}
 m\leq N_i,\quad N_i-m\leq k,\quad N_i-m\leq3\varepsilon n,\quad
 (3+4\Delta^2)\varepsilon n\leq aN_i,\\
 (d/2)n\leq(d-\varepsilon)N_i,\qquad
 \varepsilon N_i\leq\varepsilon'm,\\
 (d'-\varepsilon')m\leq(d-\varepsilon)N_i-3\varepsilon n,\qquad
 (d'-\varepsilon')m\leq(d-\varepsilon)N_i-3\varepsilon n
                                     -4\Delta^2\varepsilon n,\\
 (d'-\varepsilon')+8\Delta^2\varepsilon/\varepsilon'\leq d-\varepsilon.
\end{gathered}
\]
It also supplies $\varepsilon\leq a$ and $\varepsilon'<\infty$.
Thus every floor, ceiling, strict-threshold transfer, density loss,
and degree margin used below has a statement-level source.

Apply \noderef{ClusterwiseDeletionBuffer} with $m,k,\Delta$, using equal
fibers, $m\leq N_i$, $N_i-m\leq k$, and the original two maximum-degree
bounds. It gives a deterministic $X$ with the exact filtered count
$|X\cap X_i|=N_i-m$. Substituting the numerical loss bound gives
$|X\cap X_i|\leq3\varepsilon n$. For the exact external buffer
\[
 E=\{x\notin X:\exists y\in X,\ xy\in E(H)\},\qquad S=X\cup E,
\]
the same theorem gives $|E\cap X_i|\leq\Delta^2k$. Casting this natural-number
inequality to the nonnegative scalars and using $k\leq4\varepsilon n$ yields
$|E\cap X_i|\leq\Delta^2k\leq4\Delta^2\varepsilon n$.
Filtering $X\cup E$ by its cluster commutes with union, and the cardinality
of a union is at most the sum of the cardinalities. Consequently
\[
 |S\cap X_i|\leq |X\cap X_i|+|E\cap X_i|
 \leq3\varepsilon n+4\Delta^2\varepsilon n
 =(3+4\Delta^2)\varepsilon n\leq aN_i.
\]
In particular the entire external buffer is
included in the restricted source, while only $X$ will be embedded.

Apply \noderef{RebalancingRestrictionEstimates} with the original
host and candidacy lower-super-regularity, $\varepsilon\leq a$,
the original size window, and $(d/2)n\leq(d-\varepsilon)N_i$.
It supplies host lower-$(a,d/2)$-regularity, the host-size window
with error $a$, and at least $(d/2)n$ original candidates at every
original source vertex. These are not degree-only substitutes for
regularity. Together with the two original degree bounds and
$|S\cap X_i|\leq aN_i$, they verify every assumption of
\noderef{RestrictedSmallBlowUpInterface}. That node supplies $B_S,A_S$
on the literal source subtype $S$, with unchanged host and reduced
graphs, restricted source map, unchanged host map, exact original
candidacy relation, and all numerical premises of
\noderef{PartialEmbedding} at $(\Delta,d/2,a,n)$.

Use the partial conclusion of \noderef{PartialEmbedding} on $B_S,A_S$
and the subset $T=\{x\in S:x\in X\}$. It gives a law $\nu$ on partial
compatible maps with reserve at least $d'n$ at every vertex of
$S\setminus T$, whose total-map law on $S$ is $(C/n)$-spread.
For every original vertex $x$ the candidate-degree bound is strictly
positive, because $d/2>0$ and $n\geq4$. Hence choose an original candidate
$g(x)$ once and for all (the choice is the empty function if the source
is empty). Apply \noderef{RestrictedPartialEmbeddingTransport} with
this $g$, the exact restricted candidacy equivalence, $X\subseteq S$,
the defining equivalence for $T$, $q=C/n$, and $\rho=d'n$.
It supplies the ambient partial law $\mu$ with spread on its restrictions
to $X$, and reserve $d'n$ on every vertex of $S\setminus X$, in particular
every vertex of $E$, for every supported ambient map. The transport
identifies the updated candidate sets in both directions and removes
exactly the same image. It claims spread only on $X$, not on the fixed
ignored ambient values.

Fix $f$ with $\mu(f)\ne0$. Apply \noderef{RebalancingResidualGeometry}
with equal original fibers, $m\leq N_i$, and $|X\cap X_i|=N_i-m$.
It constructs $B_r,A_r$ on the literal source and host complements,
the induced graphs, and the unchanged reduced graph. Its two cluster
maps are the restrictions of the originals; its allowed relation is
exactly the ambient \noderef{UpdatedCandidacy} relation, in both directions.
Both residual fibers have size $m$, and it also proves
$|f(X)\cap V_i|=|X\cap X_i|\leq3\varepsilon n$.

For the host apply \noderef{RebalancingResidualHost} with
$L=3\varepsilon n$. The exact host-map identity and host fiber size
come from geometry, original host super-regularity is assumed, and
the removed-host bound was just proved. Its threshold-transfer
and degree-margin hypotheses are exactly the displayed numerical
bounds. Its density hypothesis follows by discarding the nonnegative
$8\Delta^2\varepsilon/\varepsilon'$ term. This proves
host lower-$(\varepsilon',d')$-super-regularity for $B_r$.

For candidacy apply \noderef{RebalancingResidualCandidacy} with
\[
 L=3\varepsilon n,\quad J=4\Delta^2\varepsilon n,\quad
 \eta=8\Delta^2\varepsilon,\quad \rho=d'n.
\]
The exact residual interfaces and both fiber sizes are supplied by
geometry; equal original fibers and original candidacy super-regularity
are assumptions. The source and host deletion bounds are at most $L$,
and the external-buffer estimate proved above gives the exceptional count at most $J$.
Since $m\geq n/2$, we have $J\leq\eta m$.
Positivity of $\varepsilon'$ is assumed and its finiteness was supplied
by the numerical node. Threshold transfer, density loss, and the full
right-degree margin are exactly the numerical bounds displayed above.
Finally $m\leq n$ and $d'-\varepsilon'\leq d'$ give
$(d'-\varepsilon')m\leq d'n=\rho$; every external-buffer vertex has
that reserve by the transported law. Thus every hypothesis is checked.
The candidacy node applies exceptional-row slicing, including ordinary
row equality and the right-degree buffer loss, and transports edge
counts, density, neighborhood counts, and fiber sizes to the literal
residual subtypes. It yields candidacy
lower-$(\varepsilon',d')$-super-regularity. These two regularity
conclusions and the exact geometry give the required conclusion
for every supported $f$.
\end{proof}
