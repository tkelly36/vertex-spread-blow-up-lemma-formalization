\begin{lemma}[Increasing the super-regularity error]
Let $G$ be a graph on a finite vertex set, let $L,R$ be vertex sets, and let
$\beta\leq\gamma$ and $d$ be nonnegative extended real numbers.
If $G[L,R]$ is $(\beta,d)$-super-regular, then it is
$(\gamma,d)$-super-regular.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Unfold \noderef{SuperRegularPair} and \noderef{RegularPair}.
If $U\subseteq L$ and $W\subseteq R$ satisfy
$|U|>\gamma|L|$ and $|W|>\gamma|R|$, then they also satisfy the
corresponding strict inequalities with $\beta$. The input regularity gives
$d-\beta\leq d_G(U,W)\leq d+\beta$. Since subtraction is truncated and
$\beta\leq\gamma$, one has $d-\gamma\leq d-\beta$ and
$d+\beta\leq d+\gamma$, proving the required regularity.
For each left vertex multiply these same endpoint comparisons by $|R|$;
for each right vertex multiply them by $|L|$. Nonnegativity preserves the
inequalities, so the two input degree bounds imply the two output degree
bounds. These are all three clauses of super-regularity.
\end{proof}
