\begin{lemma}[Lower super-regularity after sampling and correction]
Let $B,A$ have $r$ original source and host clusters of common size $n$,
with lower $(\varepsilon,d)$-super-regular host and candidacy pairs.
Let $k\geq1$, $n\geq2k$, and let $\sigma$ refine the source map into
fibers of sizes $m$ or $m+1$, $m=\lfloor n/k\rfloor$. Let $f,f'$
refine the host map with exactly the same capacities. Suppose each
$f'$-bin differs from its $f$-bin in at most $2k\varepsilon n$ positions.
Suppose every source vertex has degree at least $(d-2\varepsilon)$
times the bin size into every $f$-bin of its original cluster;
every original host vertex has the same bound into every $f$-bin
of each adjacent original cluster; and every host vertex has at least
$(d-2\varepsilon)$ times the size of its assigned $\sigma$-bin
as candidacy degree into that source bin. Assume
\[
 \theta<1,\qquad (4k^2+2)\varepsilon\leq\theta,
 \qquad 2k\varepsilon\leq\theta.
\]
Then every refined host pair whose original indices are adjacent in
$R$ is lower $(\theta,d)$-super-regular, and every corresponding
tagged source/$f'$-host candidacy pair is lower
$(\theta,d)$-super-regular. Counts and parameters use extended
nonnegative reals, with truncated lower endpoints.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The displayed smallness bound implies $\varepsilon\leq\theta<1$,
so $\varepsilon$ is finite. Also $m\geq n/(2k)>0$.
For a refined capacity $s\geq m$,
$\varepsilon n\leq2k\varepsilon s\leq\theta s$.
Each source or corrected host bin is contained in its original bin,
by its projection identity. Thus the original lower regularity
transfers to the refined parts by \noderef{LowerRegularRestriction}.
For candidacy use the disjoint tagged copies; their inclusions and
cardinalities are precisely the ordinary fiber inclusions and sizes.
Original lower regularity is supplied by
\noderef{InstanceLowerSuperRegular} and
\noderef{CandidacyLowerSuperRegular}, unpacked with
\noderef{LowerSuperRegularPair}.

For any fixed vertex and any predicate specifying its neighbors,
its old neighbor count is at most its new neighbor count plus the
bin symmetric-difference size. Indeed old neighbors not still in
the new bin inject into the old bin minus the new bin, a subset
of the symmetric difference. The two capacities are equal.
Consequently, from an old degree bound $(d-2\varepsilon)s$,
the new count is at least $(d-2\varepsilon)s-2k\varepsilon n$
in real notation when $d$ is finite and $d>2\varepsilon$.
Since $n\leq2ks$, the loss is at most $4k^2\varepsilon s$,
and this gives degree at least $(d-\theta)s$ on both host
orientations and for every refined source vertex into its host bin.
The host estimates apply to all original vertices, including those
which changed bins. The hypothesis on the assigned source bin
directly supplies reverse candidacy degrees; weakening
$2\varepsilon\leq\theta$ gives the desired lower bound.
Tagging preserves these degree counts: an edge from an inl endpoint
to an inr endpoint is exactly an allowed pair, with no reversed-tag
branch, so all degree clauses of \noderef{LowerSuperRegularPair}
hold. Together with the transferred regularity this proves both claims.

For completeness this argument remains valid with all extended
parameters in the statement. If $d$ is finite and $d\leq\theta$,
the requested degree lower endpoint is zero and no real subtraction
is needed. If $d>\theta$, it is a finite positive endpoint and the
calculation above applies. If $d=\top$ and any original required
pair or candidacy cluster exists, its positive opposite cardinality
$n$ and finite $\varepsilon$ would make its prescribed minimum degree
infinite, contradicting the finite neighbor count. Thus with $d=\top$
there can be no such original pair or candidacy cluster; the asserted
families are vacuous. Densities in the finite-$d$ cases are the same
ordered-edge ratios before and after taking tagged copies, by
\noderef{PairDensity}. Empty index sets are also vacuous.
\end{proof}
