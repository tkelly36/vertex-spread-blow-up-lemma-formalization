\begin{lemma}[The uniform subset intersection law]
Let $T\subseteq S$ be finite sets and $s\leq |S|$. For every
nonnegative integer $x$, the fraction of $s$-subsets $U$ of $S$
with $|U\cap T|=x$ equals $H(|S|,s,|T|,x)$, the mass defined
in \noderef{HypergeometricMass}.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
There are $\binom{|S|}{s}>0$ such subsets, including when $S$ is
empty (then $s=0$). If $x>s$, none has intersection size $x$, and
the defining conditional branch of \noderef{HypergeometricMass}
is zero. If $x\leq s$, the map
$U\mapsto(U\cap T,U\setminus T)$ is a bijection between the
subsets being counted and pairs consisting of an $x$-subset of $T$
and an $(s-x)$-subset of $S\setminus T$. Its inverse is union;
the two chosen sets are disjoint, their union has size $s$, and its
intersection with $T$ is exactly the first chosen set. The number
of pairs is
$\binom{|T|}{x}\binom{|S|-|T|}{s-x}$, since $T\subseteq S$.
This also gives zero in the infeasible cases, by the vanishing of
the relevant binomial coefficient. Divide by the positive total
number to obtain the formula in \noderef{HypergeometricMass}.
For interpretation as probability, Mathlib's \texttt{PMF.toOuterMeasure\_uniformOfFinset\_apply}
says that the uniform finite law assigns exactly this fraction to
the event. The proof of the equality itself is the counting bijection,
so no tail or independence assumption is being used.
\end{proof}
