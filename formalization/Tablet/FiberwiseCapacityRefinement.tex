\begin{lemma}[Refining finite capacities by colors]
Let $X,V$ be finite sets, $I,J$ be index sets, $a:X\to I$ and
$b:V\to I$ be total maps, $c:X\to J$ be a coloring, and $j_0\in J$.
Suppose that for every $i\in I$ an injection
$e_i:a^{-1}(i)\to b^{-1}(i)$ is given.
Then there is a total map $d:V\to J$ such that, for every $i\in I,j\in J$,
\[
 |\{x:a(x)=i,\ c(x)=j\}|\leq
 |\{v:b(v)=i,\ d(v)=j\}|.
\]
No equality of the original capacities, nonempty fiber, or ordering of
$V$ is required.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
For $x\in X$, define $f(x)$ to be the underlying vertex of
$e_{a(x)}(x)$.  Then $b(f(x))=a(x)$.
If $f(x)=f(y)$, applying $b$ gives $a(x)=a(y)=i$.
Both images can therefore be compared in the same fiber $b^{-1}(i)$,
where injectivity of $e_i$ gives $x=y$.  Thus $f:X\to V$ is injective.
For $v\in f(X)$, let $x$ be its unique preimage and set $d(v)=c(x)$.
For $v\notin f(X)$, set $d(v)=j_0$.  This defines $d$ on all of $V$,
and $d(f(x))=c(x)$ for every $x$.
Consequently $f$ restricts to an injection from
$\{x:a(x)=i,c(x)=j\}$ to $\{v:b(v)=i,d(v)=j\}$.
The cardinality inequality for this injection gives the assertion at
every pair $(i,j)$.  Empty fibers give empty restricted injections, and
unused host vertices are covered by the default color.
\end{proof}
