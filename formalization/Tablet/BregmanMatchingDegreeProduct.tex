\begin{lemma}[Matching row-degree product]
Let $G$ be a finite simple graph, let $S,T$ be disjoint finite sets, and
let $e_S:S\to\{0,\ldots,y-1\}$ and $e_T:T\to\{0,\ldots,y-1\}$ be
bijections. Put $r_i=|\{w\in T:G(e_S^{-1}(i),w)\}|$ and let $m$ be
the number of perfect matchings from $S$ to $T$. If some $r_i=0$, then
$m=0$. If every $r_i>0$, then
\[
 m\le\prod_{i=0}^{y-1}(r_i!)^{1/r_i}.
\]
The empty product is one, so the positive-row assertion includes $y=0$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Form the natural-number matrix $A_{ij}=1$ when
$G(e_S^{-1}(i),e_T^{-1}(j))$ holds and $A_{ij}=0$ otherwise.
Every entry is either zero or one, which is exactly the hypothesis
\texttt{ZeroOneMatrix} of the checked imported theorem
\texttt{EntropyMethodsFormalization.BregmanMinc}. Its row support is
$\{j:A_{ij}=1\}$; the bijection $e_T^{-1}$ identifies this set with the
neighbors of $e_S^{-1}(i)$ in $T$, so its cardinality is $r_i$.

Casting the permanent from natural numbers to real numbers commutes with
its finite sum of finite products. The cast matrix is the real zero-one
adjacency matrix. By \noderef{AdjacencyPermanentMatchingCount}, its
permanent is $m$, with perfect matchings interpreted as in
\noderef{PerfectMatching}. Thus the natural-number permanent also equals
$m$, since the natural-to-real map is injective. Apply the zero-row branch
of the imported Bregman--Minc theorem when a row support is empty, and
its positive-row branch otherwise. These give respectively $m=0$ and
the stated product inequality. No entropy argument is required locally.
\end{proof}
