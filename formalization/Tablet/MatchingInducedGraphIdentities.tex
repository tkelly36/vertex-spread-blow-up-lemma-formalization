\begin{lemma}[Copied matching-induced degree and codegree identities]
Let $F,H$ be graphs on a finite vertex set, let $U,W,Z$ be finite parts,
and let $f:U\to W$ be any function. Put
$A=\noderef{MatchingInducedGraph}(F,H,U,W,Z,f)$, with copied parts
$L=\{\operatorname{inl}x:x\in U\}$ and
$R=\{\operatorname{inr}z:z\in Z\}$. Then $|L|=|U|$, $|R|=|Z|$,
$L\cap R=\varnothing$, and
\[
 A(\operatorname{inl}x,\operatorname{inr}z)
 \iff F(x,z)\ \text{and}\ H(f(x),z).
\]
In particular, the degree of $\operatorname{inl}x$ into $R$ is
$|\{z\in Z:F(x,z)\land H(f(x),z)\}|$, the degree of
$\operatorname{inr}z$ into $L$ is
$|\{x\in U:F(x,z)\land H(f(x),z)\}|$, and for every ordered pair
$(z,z')\in Z^2$, including diagonal pairs, its common-neighborhood size
in $L$ is
\[
 |\{x\in U:(F(x,z)\land F(x,z'))\land
                    (H(f(x),z)\land H(f(x),z'))\}|.
\]
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The two injections into a disjoint sum preserve cardinalities and have
disjoint images. By the definition in \noderef{MatchingInducedGraph},
an edge between the indicated copies can only arise from the forward
generating relation: its reverse would identify a right copy with a left
copy. Injectivity of each inclusion forces its witnesses to be exactly
$x,z$, giving the displayed adjacency equivalence. For the left degree,
the map $\operatorname{inr}z\mapsto z$ is a bijection between the filtered
copied part and the stated subset of $Z$. For the right degree,
$\operatorname{inl}x\mapsto x$ gives the corresponding bijection.
Applying the same bijection with both adjacency conditions gives the
codegree identity. This uses no inequality or distinctness condition on
$z,z'$, so includes the diagonal.
\end{proof}
