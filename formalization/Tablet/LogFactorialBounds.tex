\begin{lemma}[Elementary logarithmic factorial bounds]
For every positive integer $n$,
\[
 n\log n-n\le\log(n!)\le n\log n-n+\log n+1.
\]
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
For every positive real number $z$ the tangent-line inequality for the
logarithm gives $\log z\le z-1$. Applying this inequality to $1/z$
also gives $1-1/z\le\log z$.
For completeness, the tangent-line inequality follows because the
function $z-1-\log z$ has derivative $1-1/z$, which is nonpositive
on $(0,1]$ and nonnegative on $[1,\infty)$; its value at $1$ is zero.
For a positive integer $n$, put
$q_n=\log(n+1)-\log n=\log((n+1)/n)$.
The two inequalities just proved yield
\[
 n q_n\le1,\qquad (n+1)q_n\ge1.
\]
We now prove the two factorial bounds simultaneously by induction.
At $n=1$, they read $-1\le0\le0$.
If the lower bound holds at $n$, then
\[
 \log((n+1)!)\ge n\log n-n+\log(n+1)
 \ge (n+1)\log(n+1)-(n+1),
\]
where the second inequality is precisely $nq_n\le1$.
If the upper bound holds at $n$, then
\[
 \log((n+1)!)\le n\log n-n+\log n+1+\log(n+1)
 \le (n+1)\log(n+1)-(n+1)+\log(n+1)+1,
\]
where the second inequality is precisely $(n+1)q_n\ge1$.
Here both induction steps use $(n+1)!=(n+1)n!$ and positivity of
its factors to take logarithms. This completes the induction.
\end{proof}
