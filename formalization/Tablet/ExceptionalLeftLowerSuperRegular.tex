\begin{lemma}[Exceptional-row slicing for a candidacy pair]
Let $(L_0,R_0)$ be a lower $(\varepsilon,d)$-super-regular pair in a graph $G_0$.  Let $D_L\subseteq L_0$ and $D_R\subseteq R_0$, put
\[
 L=L_0\setminus D_L,\qquad R=R_0\setminus D_R,
\]
and let $E\subseteq L$.  Let $G$ be an updated graph such that, for every $x\in L\setminus E$ and $y\in R$,
\[
 xy\in E(G)\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad xy\in E(G_0).
\]
Suppose that $0<\varepsilon'<\infty$, $|E|\leq\eta|L|$, and
\[
 \varepsilon|L_0|\leq\varepsilon'|L|,
 \qquad
 \varepsilon|R_0|\leq\varepsilon'|R|,
 \qquad
 (d'-\varepsilon')+\frac{\eta}{\varepsilon'}\leq d-\varepsilon.
\]
Suppose also that
\[
 (d'-\varepsilon')|R|\leq (d-\varepsilon)|R_0|-|D_R|
\]
and
\[
 (d'-\varepsilon')|L|\leq (d-\varepsilon)|L_0|-|D_L|-|E|.
\]
Finally, suppose that every exceptional vertex $x\in E$ has at least
$(d'-\varepsilon')|R|$ neighbors in $R$ in $G$.  Then $(L,R)$ is lower
$(\varepsilon',d')$-super-regular in $G$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Write $e_J(S,T)$ for the number of ordered edges from $S$ to $T$ in a graph
$J$, as in \noderef{PairDensity}.  We verify separately the three clauses of
\noderef{LowerSuperRegularPair}.

First consider an ordinary vertex $x\in L\setminus E$.  Lower
$(\varepsilon,d)$-super-regularity of $(L_0,R_0)$ gives
\[
 |N_{G_0}(x)\cap R_0|\geq(d-\varepsilon)|R_0|.
\]
Passing from $R_0$ to the exact residual part $R=R_0\setminus D_R$ deletes at
most $|D_R|$ of these neighbors.  The exact ordinary-row equality between
$G$ and $G_0$ on $R$ and the first degree-margin inequality therefore give
\[
 |N_G(x)\cap R|\geq(d-\varepsilon)|R_0|-|D_R|
 \geq(d'-\varepsilon')|R|.
\]
For $x\in E$ the same conclusion is precisely the exceptional-row hypothesis.
Thus every vertex of $L$ has the required degree into $R$.

Next fix $y\in R$.  In $G_0$ it has at least
$(d-\varepsilon)|L_0|$ neighbors in $L_0$.  At most $|D_L|$ of these lie in
the deleted left part.  On the remaining ordinary rows $L\setminus E$, the
adjacency relation is unchanged; discarding all $|E|$ exceptional rows covers
every possible further loss caused by the update.  Consequently
\[
 |N_G(y)\cap L|\geq(d-\varepsilon)|L_0|-|D_L|-|E|
 \geq(d'-\varepsilon')|L|.
\]
This proves the right-degree clause.

It remains to prove lower regularity rather than assume it.  Take
$S\subseteq L$ and $T\subseteq R$ with
$|S|>\varepsilon'|L|$ and $|T|>\varepsilon'|R|$.  The two threshold-transfer
inequalities imply $|S|>\varepsilon|L_0|$ and
$|T|>\varepsilon|R_0|$.  Lower regularity of the original pair, which is part
of its lower-super-regularity, hence yields
\[
 e_{G_0}(S,T)\geq(d-\varepsilon)|S||T|.
\]
Every ordered edge whose left endpoint is in $S\setminus E$ is preserved in
$G$, so at most $|S\cap E||T|\leq |E||T|$ ordered edges can be lost.  Since
$|E|\leq\eta|L|$ and $|S|>\varepsilon'|L|$, positivity of $\varepsilon'$ gives
$|E|/|S|\leq\eta/\varepsilon'$.  Dividing by $|S||T|$ gives
\[
 d_G(S,T)\geq d-\varepsilon-\frac{\eta}{\varepsilon'}
 \geq d'-\varepsilon',
\]
where the last inequality is the density-margin hypothesis.  Thus $(L,R)$ is
lower $(\varepsilon',d')$-regular.  This regularity conclusion and the two
degree conclusions are exactly the three clauses of
\noderef{LowerSuperRegularPair}, completing the proof.
\end{proof}
