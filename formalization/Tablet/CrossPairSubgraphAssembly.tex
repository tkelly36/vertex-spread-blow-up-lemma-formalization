\begin{lemma}[Assembly of contained crossing subgraphs]
Let $G$ be a finite simple graph, $R$ a simple graph on a finite index
type, and $f$ assign each vertex of $G$ an index. Suppose for every
ordered edge $pq$ of $R$ there is a graph $F\subseteq G$, with
edges only crossing the fibers of $p,q$, which is
$(\eta,c)$-super-regular on those fibers. There is a graph
$G'\subseteq G$ on the original vertex type, supported only on
$R$-edges between fibers, whose pair on every edge of $R$ is
$(\eta,c)$-super-regular.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Choose a linear ordering of the finite index type, for example by
an enumeration, and for every unordered edge $\{p,q\}$ select just
one of the supplied graphs, using the orientation $p<q$.
Let $G'$ be the union of their edge relations. Each selected relation
is symmetric and irreflexive; so is their union. Each relation is
contained in $G$, and each of its edges has endpoint fibers equal
to its selected index pair. Hence $G'\subseteq G$ and its edges
project to edges of $R$.

For a fixed edge $pq$ and vertices $u\in f^{-1}(p)$,
$v\in f^{-1}(q)$, any selected graph contributing the edge $uv$
must have unordered index pair exactly $\{p,q\}$: this follows
from its crossing support and uniqueness of the values $f(u),f(v)$.
Conversely the selected graph for this pair contributes all its
edges to $G'$. Thus on this pair the assembled graph agrees
exactly with that one selected graph. In particular every opposite
degree count and every subset ordered-edge count is identical;
so are the densities by \noderef{PairDensity}. Transfer along this
agreement using \noderef{SuperRegularPairEmbedding}, with the identity
map on the vertex type, the selected graph as source, and $G'$ as target.
The identity is injective, its images of the two finite fibers are
the fibers themselves, and the crossing-edge equivalence just proved
is exactly the embedding's adjacency hypothesis. The parameters
$\eta,c$ are unchanged. If the tested
orientation is the one selected, the supplied
\noderef{SuperRegularPair} assertion applies directly. In the other
orientation its two degree clauses exchange places, and the
regularity assertion exchanges the two subset variables. Their
densities are equal, because coordinate swap is a bijection of
the corresponding ordered-edge sets. Thus the same assertion holds
in both orientations. Empty fiber tests and truncated or infinite
scalar endpoints cause no additional issue: agreement is equality
of the counts, not an estimate or real-number conversion.
\end{proof}
