\begin{lemma}[Simultaneous right degrees from set-image concentration]
Let $G,F,H$ be graphs on a finite vertex set, $U,W,Z$ finite parts with $U,W$ disjoint,
$|U|=|W|=m>0$, and let $\mu$ be any probability mass function on perfect
matchings $f:U\to W$ of $G$, as in \noderef{PerfectMatching}.
All scalar parameters below lie in the extended nonnegative reals.
Suppose $(U,Z)$ in $F$ and $(W,Z)$ in $H$ are respectively
$(\varepsilon,x)$- and $(\varepsilon,y)$-super-regular,
\[
 x/2\leq x-\varepsilon,\quad y/2\leq y-\varepsilon,\quad
 xy-\delta\leq(x-\varepsilon)(y-\varepsilon)-\zeta,\quad
 (x+\varepsilon)(y+\varepsilon)+\zeta\leq xy+\delta.
\]
Suppose for every $S\subseteq U,T\subseteq W$ with
$|S|\geq(x/2)m$, $|T|\geq(y/2)m$, the event
\[
 |S||T|/m-\zeta m\leq |\{u\in S:f(u)\in T\}|
 \leq |S||T|/m+\zeta m
\]
has $\mu$-probability at least $1-c^m$.
Then, with probability at least $1-|Z|c^m$, every right vertex of
$A_f=\noderef{MatchingInducedGraph}(F,H,U,W,Z,f)$ has degree into its
copied left part in $[(xy-\delta)m,(xy+\delta)m]$.
All subtractions are truncated at zero.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
For $z\in Z$ set $S_z=\{u\in U:F(u,z)\}$ and
$T_z=\{w\in W:H(w,z)\}$. By the right-degree clauses of
\noderef{SuperRegularPair},
\[
 (x-\varepsilon)m\leq|S_z|\leq(x+\varepsilon)m,\qquad
 (y-\varepsilon)m\leq|T_z|\leq(y+\varepsilon)m.
\]
The first two scalar inequalities therefore check the two lower set-size
hypotheses of the assumed concentration estimate. The right-degree
identity in \noderef{MatchingInducedGraphIdentities} says explicitly
\[
 \deg_{A_f}(\operatorname{inr}z)
 =|\{u\in U:F(u,z)\land H(f(u),z)\}|
 =|\{u\in S_z:f(u)\in T_z\}|.
\]
Write $b_z=|S_z||T_z|/m$. Multiplication of the cardinality bounds and
cancellation of the positive finite $m$ give
\[
 (x-\varepsilon)(y-\varepsilon)m\leq b_z
 \leq(x+\varepsilon)(y+\varepsilon)m.
\]
This remains valid for extended parameters: the lower factors are finite
because their products with the positive $m$ are bounded by finite
cardinalities; an infinite upper factor only weakens the upper bound.
Multiplication by the finite $m$ commutes with truncated subtraction.
Thus the two scalar window assumptions imply
$(xy-\delta)m\leq b_z-\zeta m$ and
$b_z+\zeta m\leq(xy+\delta)m$. Each concentration success therefore
implies the desired degree window for $z$.

For completeness, in any probability space if an event $E$ has probability
at least $1-t$ (truncated subtraction), then its complement has probability
at most $t$: for $t\geq1$ this is automatic, and for $t<1$ it follows
from $\Pr(E)+\Pr(E^c)=1$. Apply this to each of the $|Z|$ events above.
The complement of their intersection is their union of complements, of
probability at most $|Z|c^m$ by finite subadditivity. Passing back to the
intersection gives the claimed lower bound; its containment in the
simultaneous degree event finishes the proof.
\end{proof}
