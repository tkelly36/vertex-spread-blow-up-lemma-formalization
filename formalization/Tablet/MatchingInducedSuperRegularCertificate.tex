\begin{lemma}[A deterministic certificate for the matching-induced graph]
Let $(G,F,H)$ on a finite vertex set with parts $(U,W,Z)$ be a near-equal
$(\varepsilon,d,x,y)$-regular triplet of order $m$, in the sense of
\noderef{NearEqualRegularTriplet}, and let $f:U\to W$ be a perfect
matching of $G$. Put $q=xy$, $k=|Z|$, and let $A_f$ be the copied graph
of \noderef{MatchingInducedGraph}, with copied parts $L,R$.
Suppose
\[
 m\geq2,\quad \varepsilon\leq\min(\delta,\eta),\quad
 \delta\leq\min(\eta,q),\quad q\leq1,\quad\eta\leq1,\quad
 1\leq\delta m,\quad32\delta\leq\eta^5.
\]
If every right vertex has degree into $L$ in
$[(q-\delta)m,(q+\delta)m]$, and at most $6\varepsilon k^2$
ordered pairs of $R^2$ have more than $(q+\delta)^2m$ common neighbors
in $L$, then $A_f$ is $(\eta,q)$-super-regular on $L,R$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
By \noderef{MatchingInducedGraphIdentities}, $|L|=m$, $|R|=k$, and
the two copied parts are disjoint. Each left vertex $u$ is matched to
$f(u)$ along an edge of $G$, by \noderef{PerfectMatching}.
The triplet common-neighborhood clause of
\noderef{NearEqualRegularTriplet}, together with the left-degree identity,
therefore gives its degree into $R$ in $[(q-\varepsilon)k,
(q+\varepsilon)k]$. Since $\varepsilon\leq\eta$, this is contained
in the required $[(q-\eta)k,(q+\eta)k]$ window. The assumed right
window is likewise contained in the target window because $\delta\leq\eta$.

In the exceptional-pair test of
\noderef{NearEqualDegreeCodegreeRegularityCriterion}, neither low-degree
disjunct can hold, by the assumed $(q-\delta)m$ right-degree bound.
Its exceptional set is thus exactly the set of excessive codegrees.
Its size is at most $6\varepsilon k^2\leq6\delta k^2$.
The criterion applies with order $m$, right size $k\in\{m-1,m,m+1\}$,
density $q$, error $\eta$, and auxiliary error $\delta$: disjointness,
both sizes, $\eta\leq1$, $\delta\leq q\leq1$, $m\geq2$,
$1\leq\delta m$, and $32\delta\leq\eta^5$ have all been checked.
It proves $(\eta,q)$-regularity. Combining this with the two degree
windows is precisely \noderef{SuperRegularPair}. Ordered pairs have
never been restricted to distinct entries.
\end{proof}
