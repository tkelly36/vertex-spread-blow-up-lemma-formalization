\begin{lemma}[Floor, ceiling, and loss margins]
Fix $\Delta\geq1$, $0<d\leq1$, $0<d'\leq d/4$, and $0<a\leq1/4$.
There exists $0<\varepsilon'_0\leq\min\{1/4,d'/2\}$ such that for each
$0<\varepsilon'\leq\varepsilon'_0$ there is $\varepsilon_0>0$ such that,
for each $0<\varepsilon\leq\varepsilon_0$ and all sufficiently large $n$,
there are integers $m,k\geq0$ satisfying $n\geq4$,
$\varepsilon\leq a,1/4,d/4$, $\varepsilon'<\infty$, and
\[
 m\leq(1-\varepsilon)n<m+1,\qquad n/2\leq m\leq n,
 \qquad k\leq4\varepsilon n,
 \qquad (d'-\varepsilon')+8\Delta^2\varepsilon/\varepsilon'\leq d-\varepsilon.
\]
For every integer $N$ in the window $(1-\varepsilon)n\leq N\leq(1+\varepsilon)n$,
\[
 m\leq N,\quad N-m\leq k,\quad N-m\leq3\varepsilon n,\quad
 (3+4\Delta^2)\varepsilon n\leq aN,\quad
 (d/2)n\leq(d-\varepsilon)N,\quad \varepsilon N\leq\varepsilon'm,
\]
and both degree margins hold:
\[
 (d'-\varepsilon')m\leq(d-\varepsilon)N-3\varepsilon n,
 \qquad
 (d'-\varepsilon')m\leq(d-\varepsilon)N-3\varepsilon n-4\Delta^2\varepsilon n.
\]
All cutoffs depend only on the displayed scalar parameters, not on the
number of clusters. Subtractions are nonnegative-real truncated subtraction.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
All input scalars are finite: $d\leq1$, $d'\leq d/4$, and $a\leq1/4$.
Work in their ordinary real representatives. Put $K=3+4\Delta^2$ and choose
\[
 \varepsilon'_0=\min\{1/4,d'/2\},\qquad
 \varepsilon_0=\min\{1/4,d/4,a,a/(2K),\varepsilon'/4,
                    d/(4K),d\varepsilon'/(32\Delta^2)\}.
\]
Every entry is strictly positive, since $\Delta\geq1$; hence both minima
are strictly positive. Fix admissible $\varepsilon$ and take
$n_0=\max\{4,\lceil1/\varepsilon\rceil\}$. Then $n\geq n_0$ implies
$\varepsilon n\geq1$. Set $m=\lfloor(1-\varepsilon)n\rfloor$ and
$k=\lceil3\varepsilon n\rceil$. The floor inequalities give
$m\leq(1-\varepsilon)n<m+1$, and
$m\geq(1-\varepsilon)n-1\geq3n/4-1\geq n/2$; also $m\leq n$.
For $N$ in the stated window, $m\leq N$, and
$N-m\leq2\varepsilon n+1\leq3\varepsilon n\leq k$.
The ceiling inequality gives $k\leq3\varepsilon n+1\leq4\varepsilon n$.

The window yields $n/2\leq N\leq2n$. Therefore
$K\varepsilon n\leq an/2\leq aN$ and
$\varepsilon N\leq2\varepsilon n\leq\varepsilon'n/2\leq\varepsilon'm$.
Since $\varepsilon\leq d/4$ and $d\leq1$,
\[
 (d-\varepsilon)(1-\varepsilon)
 =d-(d+1)\varepsilon+\varepsilon^2
 \geq d-2\varepsilon\geq d/2.
\]
Multiplying by $n$ proves the candidate-degree bound. Moreover
\[
 (d-\varepsilon)N-K\varepsilon n
 \geq(d/2-K\varepsilon)n\geq(d/4)n
 \geq(d'-\varepsilon')m.
\]
Here $K\varepsilon\leq d/4$, $d'\leq d/4$, and $m\leq n$ justify the
last two comparisons. This proves the stronger degree margin; omitting
the nonnegative buffer loss proves the other. The stronger inequality
also shows that its subtractions do not cross zero, so the same assertions
hold for truncated subtraction. Finally
$8\Delta^2\varepsilon/\varepsilon'\leq d/4$, whence
$(d'-\varepsilon')+8\Delta^2\varepsilon/\varepsilon'\leq d'+d/4
\leq d/2\leq d-\varepsilon$. Returning to extended nonnegative reals
preserves every displayed inequality and proves the claim.
\end{proof}
