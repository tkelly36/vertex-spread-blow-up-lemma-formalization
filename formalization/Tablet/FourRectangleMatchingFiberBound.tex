\begin{lemma}[Counting four-rectangle matching fibers]
Let $G$ be a graph on a finite vertex set, with disjoint parts $U,W$ of
size $n$, and $S\subseteq U$, $T\subseteq W$. Write $s=|S|$, $t=|T|$.
Let $x\le s,t$ and $t-x\le n-s$. Suppose $K(y)\ge0$ for $0\le y\le n$
and every balanced pair $A\subseteq U$, $B\subseteq W$ of order $y$ has
at most $K(y)$ perfect matchings, in the sense of
\noderef{PerfectMatching}. Then the number $M_x$ of perfect matchings
$f:U\to W$ with $|f(S)\cap T|=x$ satisfies
\[
 M_x\le\binom s x\binom t x\binom{n-t}{s-x}\binom{n-s}{t-x}
 K(x)K(s-x)K(t-x)K(n-s-(t-x)).
\]
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
By \noderef{PerfectMatching}, each counted map is a bijection and uses
only graph edges. For such a map define
$A=\{u\in S:f(u)\in T\}$, $B=f(A)$,
$C=f(S\setminus A)$, and $E=f^{-1}(T\setminus B)$.
These sets have sizes $x,x,s-x,t-x$, respectively; they lie in
$S,T,W\setminus T,U\setminus S$, respectively. There are exactly
$\binom s x\binom t x\binom{n-t}{s-x}\binom{n-s}{t-x}$ possible
quadruples $(A,B,C,E)$, by choosing subsets of the specified sizes
independently in those four fixed sets.

For a fixed quadruple, restriction sends a counted map to four perfect
matchings on the balanced pairs
\[
 (A,B),\quad (S\setminus A,C),\quad(E,T\setminus B),\quad
 (U\setminus(S\cup E),W\setminus(T\cup C)).
\]
Their orders are $x,s-x,t-x,n-s-(t-x)$. The left sets partition $U$
and the right sets partition $W$. Thus the four restrictions determine
the map uniquely. Conversely their union is a bijection using graph
edges and has exactly $x$ images of $S$ in $T$. This also works for empty
parts: the unique empty map is a perfect matching. The assumed rectangle
bound therefore bounds the size of this fiber by the product of the four
$K$ values. All four orders lie between zero and $n$, so these bounds are
nonnegative and can be multiplied. Summing over the possible quadruples
proves the displayed inequality (in fact the restriction construction
gives an exact sum of the four matching-count products).
\end{proof}
