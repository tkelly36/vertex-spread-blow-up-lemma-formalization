\begin{lemma}[Simultaneous matching completion]
Let $\Delta,r,n$ be nonnegative integers with $1\leq\Delta$, and let
$B=(H,G,R,(X_i)_{i\in[r]},(V_i)_{i\in[r]})$ be a finite blow-up
instance in the sense of \noderef{BlowUpInstance}.  Let
$c\colon V(H)\to[\Delta^2]$ be a coloring of the source vertices.  Suppose that every fiber
\[
 X_{i,j}=\{x\in X_i:c(x)=j\}
\]
has cardinality $\lfloor n/\Delta^2\rfloor$ or $\lfloor n/\Delta^2\rfloor+1$, and that whenever distinct vertices $x,y$ lie in the same original source cluster and satisfy $c(x)=c(y)$, they are nonadjacent in $H$ and have no common neighbor in $H$.  If $\Delta(R)\leq\Delta$, then there are graphs $H'\supseteq H$ and $R'$ on $[r]\times[\Delta^2]$ and a refined blow-up instance with source clusters $X_{i,j}$ such that
\[
 (i,j)(i',j')\in E(R')\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad ii'\in E(R),
\]
$\Delta(R')\leq\Delta^3$, and $H'[X_{i,j},X_{i',j'}]$ is a saturating matching in the sense of \noderef{SaturatingSourcePair} for every edge of $R'$.  No edge is added between a pair corresponding to a nonedge of $R'$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Write $k=\Delta^2$, and write $a_H$ and $a_G$ for the source and host
cluster maps of $B$ from \noderef{BlowUpInstance}.  Thus
$X_i=a_H^{-1}(i)$ and $V_i=a_G^{-1}(i)$, and these fibers partition
their respective vertex sets, with $|X_i|\leq|V_i|$ for every $i$.
Let $P=[r]\times[k]$, and put $X_p=X_{i,j}$ for $p=(i,j)$.
Every source vertex belongs to the unique part indexed by
$(a_H(x),c(x))$.

We first construct the refined host map, independently of adding source
edges.  For each $i$, choose an injection $e_i:X_i\to V_i$ using the
capacity inequality.  Explicitly, enumerate the two finite sets as
$x_1,\ldots,x_a$ and $v_1,\ldots,v_b$, where $a\leq b$, and send
$x_\ell$ to $v_\ell$ for $1\leq\ell\leq a$.  For $a=0$ this is the
empty injection.  There are only finitely many indices, so make these
choices for all $i$.  Since $1\leq\Delta$, we have $k\geq1$; fix a
distinguished color $j_0\in[k]$ (the element $0$ of $\mathrm{Fin}(k)$
in Lean).

Apply \noderef{FiberwiseCapacityRefinement} to the total maps
$a_H,a_G,c$, the injections $e_i$, and the default color $j_0$.
It gives a total host coloring $d$ such that, on defining
\[
 V_{i,j}=\{v:a_G(v)=i,\ d(v)=j\},
\]
we have $|X_{i,j}|\leq|V_{i,j}|$ for every pair $(i,j)$.
These sets are pairwise disjoint: membership in two of them forces both
indices to agree, since $a_G$ and $d$ are functions.  Every $v\in V_i$
belongs to $V_{i,d(v)}$, so they partition $V_i$; over all $i$ they
partition the whole host vertex set.  The cited capacity-refinement
statement does not presume $|X_i|=|V_i|$, and applies to empty fibers.
If $V_i$ is empty, original capacity forces $X_i$ to be empty as well.

Define $R'$ on $P$ by $R'.\mathrm{Adj}((i,j),(i',j'))$ if and only if
$R.\mathrm{Adj}(i,i')$.  Symmetry follows from symmetry of $R$, and a
loop would give a loop of $R$, so this is a simple graph.  For an edge
$pq$ of $R'$, the original parts of $p$ and $q$ are distinct, so $X_p$
and $X_q$ are disjoint.  The graph $H[X_p,X_q]$ is a matching: if
$x_1,x_2\in X_p$ were distinct and adjacent to the same $y\in X_q$,
they would have the same original cluster and color and would share
the neighbor $y$, contradicting the stated hypothesis.  Applying that
hypothesis to two distinct vertices of $X_q$ excludes a common neighbor
on the $X_p$ side.  For any $p,q$ with $R'.\mathrm{Adj}(p,q)$ false,
an $H$-edge between their parts would contradict the reduced-support
field of \noderef{BlowUpInstance}; thus no such edge exists.

Order $P$ by the images of its elements under
$E=\texttt{finProdFinEquiv}:P\simeq\mathrm{Fin}(rk)$.
Represent each unordered edge uniquely as $(p,q)$ with $E(p)<E(q)$.
For this representative, choose $S=X_p,T=X_q$ if $|X_p|\leq|X_q|$;
otherwise choose $S=X_q,T=X_p$.  In particular, a tie is always
resolved in favor of $X_p$.  Let $M\subseteq S\times T$ be the
original adjacency relation.  The two uniqueness conditions just
proved, together with $|S|\leq|T|$, are exactly the hypotheses of
\noderef{FinitePartialMatchingCompletion}.  Its conclusion gives an
injection $\tau:S\to T$ retaining every pair of $M$.
Use precisely the graph of $\tau$ as the completed bipartite relation
$C_{p,q}$.  It is a matching saturating $S$: every $s$ has partner
$\tau(s)$, uniqueness on $S$ is functional uniqueness, and uniqueness
on $T$ is injectivity of $\tau$.  Every original edge is retained by
the cited conclusion.  Empty parts are permitted by that statement.
The argument works for arbitrary finite part sizes, hence in particular
for the given floor and floor-plus-one sizes, including zero.

Choose this completion once for each unordered edge of $R'$, and let
$H'$ have all edges of $H$ together with all the added edges.
More explicitly, vertices $x,y$, in parts $p,q$ respectively, are
adjacent precisely when $R'.\mathrm{Adj}(p,q)$ and either
$E(p)<E(q)$ with $(x,y)\in C_{p,q}$, or $E(q)<E(p)$ with
$(y,x)\in C_{q,p}$.
This defines a simple graph: the added pairs have endpoints in
disjoint parts and are treated as unordered pairs, so adjacency is
symmetric and there are no loops.  The refined source parts partition
the source vertex set.  Thus the unordered pair of parts containing
the endpoints of an edge is unique.  Completions for different
unordered pairs cannot add an edge between the parts of another pair.
It follows that $H'[X_p,X_q]$ is exactly the chosen completed matching
for each edge $pq$ of $R'$, and is empty for every nonedge.  Also
$H\leq H'$ by its definition.  The adjacency relations of $R'$ and
$H'$ are decidable: the former uses the given decidable adjacency of
$R$, and the latter is membership in a finite union of the given old
edges and the finite lists of new edges.  Classical finite choices
above require no additional hypothesis on the host vertex type.

To construct the precise blow-up instance in the conclusion, let
\[
 E:\mathrm{Fin}(r)\times\mathrm{Fin}(k)\ \simeq
   \mathrm{Fin}(rk)
\]
be the bijection \texttt{finProdFinEquiv}.  Use the reduced graph
$\widetilde R=R'.\mathrm{comap}(E^{-1})$, whose adjacency at $u,w$ is
$R'.\mathrm{Adj}(E^{-1}(u),E^{-1}(w))$, and define the two cluster maps by
\[
 a'_H(x)=E(a_H(x),c(x)),\qquad
 a'_G(v)=E(a_G(v),d(v)).
\]
Both maps are total.  Since $E$ is bijective, the source fiber at
$E(i,j)$ is exactly $X_{i,j}$ and the host fiber there is exactly
$V_{i,j}$.  We now verify every field of \noderef{BlowUpInstance}.
First, each source fiber is independent in $H'$: it is a subset of
the original independent $X_i$, hence has no old $H$-edge, and every
added edge joins parts over distinct original clusters, hence cannot
have both endpoints in that fiber.  Second, its capacity inequality
is the injection inequality already proved.  This checks every index
of $\mathrm{Fin}(rk)$ because it is $E(i,j)$ for a unique pair.
Third, if $xy$ is an old edge, original reduced support gives
$R.\mathrm{Adj}(a_H(x),a_H(y))$, which gives
$R'.\mathrm{Adj}((a_H(x),c(x)),(a_H(y),c(y)))$.  If it is an added
edge, its pair of source parts was an edge of $R'$ by construction.
In either case this is exactly
$\widetilde R.\mathrm{Adj}(a'_H(x),a'_H(y))$, by the inverse identities
for $E$.  This verifies reduced support.  These are all the fields;
the host graph remains $G$, and \noderef{BlowUpInstance} requires no
further edge condition on $G$.  Denote the resulting instance by $B'$.
The inverse identity also gives
$E^{-1}(B'.\mathrm{sourceCluster}(x))=(a_H(x),c(x))$, as required.
For each edge $pq$ of $R'$, identifying the fibers at $E(p),E(q)$
with $X_p,X_q$ transports the completed injection $\tau$ to those
fibers.  The exact adjacency description gives the first disjunct
of \noderef{SaturatingSourcePair} if $X_p$ was chosen as $S$, and the
second disjunct if $X_q$ was chosen as $S$.

Finally, the neighbors of $(i,j)$ in $R'$ are precisely the pairs
$(i',j')$ with $i'\in N_R(i)$ and arbitrary $j'\in[k]$.  The bijection
with $N_R(i)\times[k]$ gives
\[
 d_{R'}(i,j)=d_R(i)k\leq\Delta k=\Delta^3,
\]
where $d_R(i)\leq\Delta(R)\leq\Delta$.  Taking the maximum over the
finite vertex set proves $\Delta(R')\leq\Delta^3$.
If $r=0$, the original cluster maps have empty codomain, so both
source and host vertex sets are empty.  The constructions of the
injections and both refined maps above are then empty constructions;
$R'$ is the graph on the empty product index set and has maximum
degree zero, while all field and matching assertions are vacuous.
Thus this case also satisfies the conclusion.  The degree estimate
corrects the symbol $R$ in line 396 of the manuscript.
\end{proof}
