\begin{lemma}[Integral augmentation along a simple residual path]
Let $f$ be a feasible integral flow on a finite integer-capacity network
with distinct terminals, and let $p(0),\ldots,p(m)$ be a simple residual
source-to-sink path, as in \noderef{SimpleResidualPath}.
There are an integer $\delta>0$ and a feasible integral flow $g$ such that
$\delta\leq c(p(i),p(i+1))-f(p(i),p(i+1))$ for every $i<m$,
\[
g(v,w)=f(v,w)+\delta\bigl(
 |\{i<m:p(i)=v,\ p(i+1)=w\}|-
 |\{i<m:p(i)=w,\ p(i+1)=v\}|\bigr),
\qquad |g|=|f|+\delta.
\]
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
By \noderef{SimpleResidualPath}, the $m$ residual capacities are positive
integers and $m>0$. Let $\delta$ be their minimum. This minimum exists
because there are finitely many and at least one; it is a positive integer
and is no greater than each residual capacity. Define $g$ by the displayed
formula. Its values are integers. Interchanging $v,w$ negates the change,
so skew symmetry follows from \noderef{FiniteIntegralFlow}.

Injectivity of $p$ implies that each directed pair occurs at most once.
An edge and its reverse cannot both occur: such occurrences would repeat
both endpoints, whereas their positions in an injective sequence are
unique. Consecutive vertices are distinct. Thus the change on any pair is
either $0$, $\delta$, or $-\delta$. On a forward path edge, the choice of
$\delta$ gives $g(v,w)\leq c(v,w)$. On a reverse path edge the flow
decreases, so its upper capacity bound is preserved. On other pairs the
bound is unchanged. This checks all upper bounds, and skew symmetry also
checks all lower bounds.

At an internal path vertex $p(j)$, $0<j<m$, its outgoing sum gains
$\delta$ on the pair to $p(j+1)$ and loses $\delta$ on the pair to
$p(j-1)$, and changes on no other pair. Its divergence is unchanged.
Vertices outside the path have unchanged divergence as well. The only
remaining path vertices are the distinct terminals. The source occurs
only as $p(0)$ and gains $\delta$ on its sole outgoing path step, with
no incoming path step. The sink has the opposite change. Hence every
required conservation equation is preserved and the value increases
by exactly $\delta$.
\end{proof}
