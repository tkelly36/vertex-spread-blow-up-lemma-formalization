\begin{lemma}[One pair-averaging step]
Let $a=(a_t)_{t\in\{1,\ldots,n\}}$ have nonnegative real entries, let
$i\ne j$, and let $k\ge0$ be an integer. Put $m=(a_i+a_j)/2$, and let
$b$ agree with $a$ except that $b_i=b_j=m$. Then $b$ has nonnegative
entries, $\sum_t b_t=\sum_t a_t$, and
\[
 \sum_{|A|=k}\prod_{t\in A}a_t
 \le \sum_{|A|=k}\prod_{t\in A}b_t.
\]
Moreover, for every real $c$,
\[
 \sum_t(b_t-c)^2=\sum_t(a_t-c)^2-\frac{(a_i-a_j)^2}{2}.
\]
All subsets in the displayed sums are subsets of $\{1,\ldots,n\}$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The new entries $m$ are nonnegative, and their sum $2m=a_i+a_j$
is the old sum of the two changed entries. Every other entry is unchanged,
which proves nonnegativity and preservation of the total sum.
For $k=0$ the only subset is empty and both product sums equal one.
For $k=1$ the product sum is the total sum of the entries, so the
claimed inequality is equality.

For $k\ge2$, put $S=\{1,\ldots,n\}\setminus\{i,j\}$ and
$E_r=\sum_{A\subseteq S,\ |A|=r}\prod_{t\in A}a_t$ for each
nonnegative integer $r$. In particular these sums are zero if $r>|S|$.
Partition the $k$-subsets into those containing neither $i$ nor $j$,
only $i$, only $j$, and both. Removing the indicated indices gives
bijections with the $k$-, $(k-1)$-, $(k-1)$-, and $(k-2)$-subsets
of $S$, respectively. In each bijection the product is multiplied
by $1,a_i,a_j$, or $a_i a_j$, respectively. Thus the old product sum is
\[
 E_k+(a_i+a_j)E_{k-1}+a_i a_j E_{k-2}.
\]
The entries on $S$ are unchanged, so the new product sum is
$E_k+2mE_{k-1}+m^2E_{k-2}$. Their difference is
\[
 (m^2-a_i a_j)E_{k-2}
 =\frac{(a_i-a_j)^2}{4}E_{k-2}\ge0,
\]
because every product defining $E_{k-2}$ is nonnegative.
No negative-index sum is used in either small-$k$ case.

Finally all terms of the squared-deviation sums except the two at
$i,j$ cancel. Expanding those two terms gives
\[
 2(m-c)^2-(a_i-c)^2-(a_j-c)^2
 =-\frac{(a_i-a_j)^2}{2},
\]
which proves the last assertion for every $c$.
\end{proof}
