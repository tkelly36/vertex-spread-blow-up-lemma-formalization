\begin{lemma}[Four Graphs parameter windows]
For $0<d_{12},d_{13},d_{23}\leq1$ and $0<\eta\leq1$, put
$q=d_{13}d_{23}$. There exist positive finite numbers $\delta,\zeta,e$
such that $\delta\leq q$, $\delta\leq\eta$,
$32\delta\leq\eta^5$, and $e\leq1$. For every $0\leq\varepsilon\leq e$,
\[
 \varepsilon\leq\min(\delta,\eta),\qquad
 2\varepsilon<\min(d_{12},d_{13},d_{23}),
\]
\[
 d_{13}/2\leq d_{13}-\varepsilon,\quad
 d_{23}/2\leq d_{23}-\varepsilon,\quad
 (d_{13}/2)^2\leq(d_{13}-2\varepsilon)^2,\quad
 (d_{23}/2)^2\leq(d_{23}-2\varepsilon)^2,
\]
and the following sharp degree and codegree windows hold:
\[
 q-\delta\leq(d_{13}-\varepsilon)(d_{23}-\varepsilon)-\zeta,
 \qquad (d_{13}+\varepsilon)(d_{23}+\varepsilon)+\zeta\leq q+\delta,
\]
\[
 (d_{13}+2\varepsilon)^2(d_{23}+2\varepsilon)^2+\zeta
 \leq(q+\delta)^2.
\]
Subtractions here are truncated at zero, as for nonnegative extended reals.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
All input numbers are finite. Work in the nonnegative reals and take
\[
 \delta=\min\{q/8,\eta/4,\eta^5/128\},\quad
 \zeta=\min\{\delta/4,q\delta/4\},\quad
 e=\min\{1/2,\delta/8,d_{12}/4,d_{13}/8,d_{23}/8,q\delta/64\}.
\]
Every entry of each minimum is strictly positive. The required bounds on
$\delta,e$ follow immediately. Fix $0\leq\varepsilon\leq e$ and write
$x=d_{13},y=d_{23}$. Then $\varepsilon\leq x/8,y/8$ gives both
lower set-density bounds and both squared lower bounds, and gives
$2\varepsilon<x,y$. Also $2\varepsilon\leq d_{12}/2<d_{12}$.
Since $x,y\leq1$ and $\varepsilon\leq1/2$,
\[
 (x-\varepsilon)(y-\varepsilon)
 =q-(x+y)\varepsilon+\varepsilon^2\geq q-2\varepsilon,
 \qquad
 (x+\varepsilon)(y+\varepsilon)\leq q+3\varepsilon.
\]
Here $2\varepsilon+\zeta\leq\delta/2$ and
$3\varepsilon+\zeta\leq5\delta/8\leq\delta$, proving the two
degree windows (and $q\geq\delta$ removes any ambiguity about their
lower endpoints). For the last window,
\[
 (x+2\varepsilon)^2\leq x^2+6\varepsilon,\qquad
 (y+2\varepsilon)^2\leq y^2+6\varepsilon,
\]
because $4x\varepsilon+4\varepsilon^2\leq6\varepsilon$.
Multiplication gives an upper bound
$q^2+12\varepsilon+36\varepsilon^2\leq q^2+30\varepsilon$.
Consequently
\[
 30\varepsilon+\zeta\leq(30/64+1/4)q\delta
 <2q\delta\leq2q\delta+\delta^2.
\]
Adding $q^2$ proves the required polynomial inequality. Embedding these
finite nonnegative real choices into the extended nonnegative reals
preserves all the stated arithmetic operations and inequalities.
\end{proof}
