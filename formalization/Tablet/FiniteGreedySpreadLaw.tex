\begin{lemma}[Finite greedy law with random total-map completion]
Let $U,V$ be finite sets, $q\in[0,1]$ an extended nonnegative real,
and $T(u)\subseteq V$ a nonempty finite set for every $u\in U$,
with $1/|T(u)|\leq q$. Let $\mathrm{Good}(S,f)$ be a predicate on
finite subsets $S\subseteq U$ and total maps $f:U\to V$ satisfying:
\begin{enumerate}
\item If $f$ and $g$ agree on $S$, then
$\mathrm{Good}(S,f)\Longleftrightarrow\mathrm{Good}(S,g)$.
\item $\mathrm{Good}(\varnothing,f)$ holds for every total map $f$.
\item Whenever $z\notin S$ and $\mathrm{Good}(S,f)$, there is a
nonempty finite set $L\subseteq V$ with $1/|L|\leq q$ such that
$\mathrm{Good}(S\cup\{z\},f[z\mapsto v])$ for every $v\in L$.
\end{enumerate}
Then for every $S\subseteq U$ there is a PMF on the subtype of total
maps satisfying $\mathrm{Good}(S,f)$ whose projection to total maps
is $q$-vertex-spread on all of $V$, in the sense of
\noderef{VertexSpreadOn}.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
First choose $b(u)\in T(u)$ for every $u$. These choices define a total
map $b$ even if $U$ is empty; no element of $V$ is needed in the empty
case. For a map $h:S\to V$, let $e_S(h)$ be the total map equal to
$h$ on $S$ and equal to $b$ outside $S$. The locality assumption
implies that whether $\mathrm{Good}(S,e_S(h))$ holds is independent
of these outside values. Call such a restricted map $h$ valid.

Fix the prescribed $S$ and list its vertices without repetition as
$z_1,\ldots,z_m$. Put $S_t=\{z_1,\ldots,z_t\}$.
We construct a law on functions $S_t\to V$ which is $q$-spread and
whose positive-mass functions are valid. For $t=0$, use the point
mass on the unique empty function. By \noderef{CylinderEvent}, its
only cylinders are of length zero, with mass $1=q^0$; an injective
positive-length prescription into the empty domain is impossible.
Validity follows from the empty
set assumption applied to $b$. Together with $q\leq1$, these cylinder
checks establish \noderef{VertexSpreadOn} on the whole codomain $V$.

Suppose the law on $S_t$ has been constructed. For every valid
function $h:S_t\to V$, apply the extension assumption to
$e_{S_t}(h)$ and $z=z_{t+1}$ and choose a corresponding set $L_h$.
On the singleton block $\{z\}$ choose its value uniformly in $L_h$.
For every invalid $h$, define the kernel instead by uniform choice
in $T(z)$. This defines a kernel for every prefix function, including
all functions of mass zero. Both choice sets are nonempty. By
Mathlib's \texttt{PMF.toOuterMeasure\_uniformOfFinset\_apply}, the probability of a prescribed
value $v$ is zero if $v$ is outside the choice set and otherwise is
its reciprocal cardinality, which is at most $q$. Mapping the chosen
value to a function on the singleton does not change this probability,
since evaluation at its sole element is an inverse to that map.
Every kernel on the singleton block is therefore $q$-spread by
\noderef{VertexSpreadOn}, using the cylinders of \noderef{CylinderEvent}:
the empty cylinder has mass one, a one-point cylinder has the bound
just proved, and an injective prescription of length at least two on
a singleton does not exist. Its scalar condition $q\leq1$ is assumed.

Apply \noderef{VertexSpreadSequential} with first source type $S_t$,
second source type $\{z\}$, and target type $V$. Its bind and map
give a $q$-spread law on functions on their disjoint sum. Identify this
sum with $S_{t+1}$ by sending each old vertex to itself and the sole
new vertex to $z$. This is a bijection because $z\notin S_t$. By
\noderef{CylinderEvent}, under precomposition by its inverse a cylinder
corresponds to a cylinder with the same number of prescriptions and
injective sources. The resulting cylinder probabilities are equal and
the scalar condition $q\leq1$ is unchanged, so
\noderef{VertexSpreadOn} is preserved.

To check support, suppose an extended function has positive mass.
All spaces are finite, so the bind and map formulas express its mass
as a finite sum of products of prefix masses and conditional masses.
If this sum is positive, at least one product is positive. Thus its
prefix has positive mass and is valid, and its new value belongs to
$L_h$: a value outside the uniform choice set has mass zero by
\texttt{PMF.toOuterMeasure\_uniformOfFinset\_apply}. The extension assumption makes
$e_{S_t}(h)[z\mapsto v]$ good on $S_{t+1}$. This total map agrees on
$S_{t+1}$ with the extension of the actual restricted function, so
locality makes that restricted function valid. The invalid-prefix
fallback is consequently never used at a positive-mass prefix.
This proves both induction assertions, yielding a law on $S$.

Next list the vertices of $U\setminus S$. Append their values one at
a time using independent uniform choices in their sets $T(u)$.
Each kernel is constant as a function of the prefix, hence is defined
on every prefix, and has the same singleton spread bound as above.
Repeated applications of \noderef{VertexSpreadSequential} and the
same cylinder-preserving bijections give a law $\nu$ on total maps
$U\to V$ satisfying \noderef{VertexSpreadOn} on the whole codomain.
Appending values never changes the restriction
to $S$. Therefore every positive-mass total map satisfies
$\mathrm{Good}(S,f)$ by the locality assumption and the established
validity of its restriction. This is why independent random values,
rather than the deterministic values of $b$, were used for the
stored completion: the desired bound concerns all source vertices.

For completeness, package this law into the subtype without changing
its projected law. The finite support
$P=\{f:\nu(f)\ne0\}$ is nonempty because the masses sum to one.
Every $f\in P$ has a proof of $\mathrm{Good}(S,f)$. Choose $f_0\in P$.
Map a total map $f$ to $\langle f,\mathrm{Good}(S,f)\rangle$ when
it belongs to $P$, and map it to the packaged $f_0$ otherwise. This
is a total function to the asserted subtype. Let $\mu$ be its
pushforward of $\nu$. For every event $E$ of total maps, the mass
of its preimage under the subtype projection is
\[
 \sum_{f\in P}\nu(f)\mathbf1_E(f)
       +\sum_{f\notin P}0\mathbf1_E(f_0)=\nu(E).
\]
Thus the projected law is exactly $\nu$. In particular, taking $E$ to
be each cylinder of \noderef{CylinderEvent} preserves every cylinder
bound, and the scalar condition $q\leq1$ is unchanged. Hence the
projected law satisfies \noderef{VertexSpreadOn} on the whole codomain
$V$. If $U$ is empty this construction is the point mass on
the empty map, even if $V$ is empty. If $S$ is empty but $U$ is not,
only the independent-completion steps are performed. Both cases
are covered by the same reasoning.
\end{proof}
