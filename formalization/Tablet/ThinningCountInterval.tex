\begin{lemma}[Deterministic count error transfer]
Let $d,\epsilon,\rho,\eta$ be finite extended nonnegative reals with
$\epsilon\leq d$, $\eta\leq\rho$. Let $q\geq0,\xi$ be real numbers
such that $qd=\rho$ and $q\epsilon+\xi\leq\eta$, where these scalar
identities use real conversions. For natural numbers $m,a,b$, if
\[
(d-\epsilon)m\leq a\leq(d+\epsilon)m,
\qquad |b-qa|\leq\xi m,
\]
then $(\rho-\eta)m\leq b\leq(\rho+\eta)m$ in the extended
nonnegative reals. This includes $m=0$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
All scalar parameters and natural counts are finite. Since
$\epsilon\leq d$, the real conversion of $d-\epsilon$ is the ordinary
difference; similarly for $\rho-\eta$. Thus convert the given interval
to real inequalities and multiply both sides by the nonnegative $q$.
The absolute-value inequality says $qa-\xi m\leq b\leq qa+\xi m$.
Combining gives
\[
(\rho-(q\epsilon+\xi))m\leq b
\leq(\rho+(q\epsilon+\xi))m.
\]
Since $m\geq0$ and $q\epsilon+\xi\leq\eta$, enlarge this interval
to the stated one. Finite real conversion preserves and reflects order,
so converting back proves the assertion, without dividing by $m$.
\end{proof}
