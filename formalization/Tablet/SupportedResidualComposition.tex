\begin{lemma}
Let $B$ be a finite blow-up instance with source $H$, host $G$, reduced graph
$R$ on $[r]$, and candidacy system $A$. Fix $X\subseteq V(H)$ and a law
$\mu$ on partial compatible embeddings with domain $X$. Suppose the law of
their restrictions to $X$ is $q$-vertex-spread on all of $V(G)$.
For every $f$ with $\mu(f)\ne0$, put
$Y=V(H)\setminus X$ and $W_f=V(G)\setminus f(X)$. Suppose there are a
blow-up instance $B_f$ on the induced graphs $H[Y],G[W_f]$ with reduced
graph $R$, and a candidacy system $A_f$, whose cluster maps are exactly the
restrictions of the original maps, and such that
\[
 A_f(y,v)\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 (\mathrm{UpdatedCandidacy}(B,A,X,f))(y,v).
\]
Suppose moreover that each such residual instance has a law on compatible
full embeddings $H[Y]\hookrightarrow G[W_f]$ that is $q$-vertex-spread
on all of $W_f$. Then there is a $q$-vertex-spread law on compatible full
embeddings $H\hookrightarrow G$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Use the meanings of partial compatibility, updated candidacy and full
compatibility in \noderef{PartialCompatibleEmbedding},
\noderef{UpdatedCandidacy} and \noderef{CompatibleEmbedding} throughout.
Restrict $\mu$ to its positive-mass support; this changes neither total mass
nor any event probability. For each supported $f$, choose one of the residual
instances and one residual law $\nu_f$ from the hypothesis. The choices are
made separately; no uniqueness of residual instances or total partial maps
is asserted. Given a sample $g$ from $\nu_f$, define
\[
 F_{f,g}(x)=\begin{cases}f(x)&x\in X,\\g(x)&x\notin X,\end{cases}
\]
where the latter value is included from the literal complement $W_f$ into
$V(G)$. The first block is injective by partial compatibility and the second
by the definition of a graph Copy. Their images are disjoint, since the
second lies outside $f(X)$. Thus $F_{f,g}$ is injective.
Edges with both endpoints in $X$ are preserved by $f$, and edges with both
endpoints in $Y$ by $g$ and the definition of induced graph. For a crossing
edge $xy$ with $x\in X,y\in Y$, compatibility of $g$ and the stipulated
updated-candidacy equivalence imply $G(F_{f,g}(y),f(x))$. Symmetry supplies
the other orientation. Hence all edges are preserved. Original candidacy
holds on $X$ by partial compatibility. On $Y$ it follows from the original
candidacy conjunct of updated candidacy. Consequently $F_{f,g}$ is a
compatible full Copy for every sample in these laws, not only almost surely.

Mix these laws with weights $\mu(f)$ on supported $f$, obtaining a law on
compatible full Copies. This is a probability law because each conditional
law has mass one and $\sum_{f:\mu(f)\ne0}\mu(f)=1$. This support-indexed
mixture avoids assigning residual laws to impossible states. Equivalently,
one can extend the resulting full-Copy kernel arbitrarily to zero-mass
states using any supported state's law; their contributions are zero.
There is a supported state because a probability law has total mass one.

Here is the joint-cylinder calculation, including the dependence on $f$.
Use \noderef{CylinderEvent} and \noderef{VertexSpreadOn}. Fix distinct
sources $x_1,\ldots,x_s$ and targets $v_1,\ldots,v_s\in V(G)$, and split
their index set into $I=\{i:x_i\in X\}$ and its complement $J$.
Write $a=|I|$, $b=|J|$, so $a+b=s$. Let $E_X$ be the event that
$f(x_i)=v_i$ for every $i\in I$. Its probability is at most $q^a$ by the
given spread law on restrictions to $X$; reindexing $I$ by $[a]$ preserves
distinctness. For a fixed supported $f$, if any target $v_j$, $j\in J$,
does not belong to $W_f$, the conditional joint-cylinder probability is
zero. Otherwise regard all these targets as elements of $W_f$. Distinctness
of the corresponding sources in $Y$ allows the residual spread bound,
so their conditional cylinder probability is at most $q^b$.
If $f\notin E_X$ the joint cylinder is impossible. Therefore its probability
under the mixture is at most
\[
 \sum_{f:\mu(f)\ne0}\mu(f)\mathbf1_{E_X}(f)q^b
 =\Pr_\mu(E_X)q^b\leq q^a q^b=q^s.
\]
All sums are of nonnegative terms, so grouping and multiplying them is valid;
the function space is finite as well. Zero-mass states contribute zero even
when distinct total partial maps have the same restriction to $X$. No
identification of those maps or unjustified conditioning is used. This is
the support-indexed bind version of the cylinder argument in
\noderef{VertexSpreadSequential}. The hypothesis on the first-stage law
also supplies $q\leq1$, including for empty source types. Packaging the
glued maps as Copies and compatible subtypes does not change their values
or cylinder probabilities. The required spread assertion follows.
\end{proof}
