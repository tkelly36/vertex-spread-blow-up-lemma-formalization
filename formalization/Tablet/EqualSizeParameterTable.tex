\begin{lemma}[Finite colour-round parameter construction]
Fix $0<d\leq1$ and $\Delta\in\mathbb N$, and put $Q=\Delta^2+1$.
There are exposure-indexed densities $p_x,\rho_x$, round-indexed errors
$\varepsilon_s,\omega_{s+1},\theta_s,\zeta_s,\widehat\zeta_s$, an induction
application certificate $\mathcal K$ with certified round bound $Q$ in the
sense of \noderef{InductionApplicationCertificate}, constants $0<a\leq1\leq M$ and
$C\geq2M$, and $n_0\in\mathbb N$ with the following properties.
One has $p_0=d$ and, for $x\leq\Delta$,
\[
 0<\rho_x<p_x\leq1,\qquad
 p_{x+1}=\rho_x(d/2)\quad(x<\Delta).
\]
For every $s<Q$,
\[
 0<\varepsilon_s<\widehat\zeta_s<\zeta_s,\quad0<\theta_s,\quad
 0<\omega_{s+1}<\varepsilon_{s+1}\leq1,\quad
 \varepsilon_s\leq\varepsilon_{s+1},
\]
\[
 2\sqrt{\zeta_s}\leq\theta_s,\quad
 \zeta_s+\Delta\sqrt{\zeta_s}\leq\theta_s,\quad
 \zeta_s+2\Delta\zeta_s\leq\theta_s,                 \tag{A1}
\]
and, for every $x\leq\Delta$ and $y<\Delta$,
\[
 4\theta_s<\rho_x,\quad 2\zeta_s<\rho_x,\quad
 2\zeta_s<1-\rho_x,\quad 2\zeta_s<d/2,\quad
 2\zeta_s<1-d/2,\quad 2\varepsilon_s<p_y-p_{y+1}.       \tag{A2}
\]
The certificate simultaneously supplies, for every $s<Q$ and every ordered
pair $x,y\leq\Delta$, the witness and host spanning-thinning conclusions,
the near-equal Four Graphs conclusion with input error $\theta_s$ and output
error $\omega_{s+1}$, perfect-matching existence, and uniform-matching
spread, exactly as specified in \noderef{InductionApplicationCertificate}.
Writing its constants and thresholds as
$a_{s,x,y},N^{\rm FG}_{s,x,y},M_{s,x},N^{\rm mat}_{s,x},
N^{\rm exist}_{s,x},N^{\rm wit}_{s,x},N^{\rm host}_s$, one has
\[
 a\leq a_{s,x,y},\qquad M_{s,x}\leq M,
\]
and every displayed threshold is at most $n_0$.  Thus one $\theta_s$ works
for all ordered exposure pairs, without any comparison between $x$ and $y$.
Finally, for every $n\geq n_0$ and $s<Q$,
\[
 \Delta(1-a)^n\leq\tfrac12,\qquad C/n\leq1,             \tag{A3}
\]
\[
 n^{-1}\leq\widehat\zeta_s,\quad
 n^{-1}\leq\zeta_s-\widehat\zeta_s,\quad
 \widehat\zeta_s+2/n\leq\zeta_s,\quad
 \widehat\zeta_s+1/(\zeta_sn)\leq\zeta_s,             \tag{A4a}
\]
\[
 \omega_{s+1}+2/n\leq\varepsilon_{s+1},\qquad
 \omega_{s+1}+1/(\varepsilon_{s+1}n)\leq\varepsilon_{s+1}. \tag{A4b}
\]
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Apply \noderef{ExposureDensityRecursion} to $d,\Delta$ to obtain
$p,\rho$ with $p_0=d$, the stated recursion, all density inequalities,
and every positive exposure gap $p_y-p_{y+1}$.

Apply \noderef{EqualSizeBackwardCertificates} to these sequences.
It gives the five round-indexed sequences and the actual induction
application certificate, all inequalities (A1)--(A2), and finite
matching constants throughout the required index ranges. Its construction
uses \noderef{EqualSizeOneRoundChoices} for each backward step: the
single-round packages use all ordered exposure pairs, and each thinning
has already been supplied by the quantitative thinning theorem. The
initial error is chosen below every host cutoff, not only its own
round's cutoff.

To apply \noderef{EqualSizeCommonOrderBounds}, verify its reserves
explicitly. For $s<Q$, $0<\varepsilon_s<\widehat\zeta_s$ implies
$\widehat\zeta_s>0$, and $\widehat\zeta_s<\zeta_s$ is already supplied.
Exposure zero is always available and gives
\[
 \zeta_s\leq2\zeta_s<\rho_0<p_0\leq1;
\]
hence $\zeta_s$ is finite. Finally
$\omega_{s+1}<\varepsilon_{s+1}\leq1$ and the finiteness of each matching
constant are outputs of the backward-certificate lemma.
The common-order lemma therefore provides $a,M,C,n_0$, all threshold
comparisons, (A3), and every bound in (A4a)--(A4b), including the separate
inner reserve $1/n\leq\widehat\zeta_s$. This proves all the required
conjuncts.

The exposure sequences were fixed from $d,\Delta$, the backward choices
use only these sequences and the $Q=\Delta^2+1$ rounds, and the common
constants and order cutoff use only the resulting finite data.
Thus no choice depends on $r$ or on a graph instance. The construction
also covers $\Delta=0$, retains $\varepsilon_Q$ for the last round, and
extends every sequence and certificate-data function to all natural
indices as stated in the backward-certificate lemma.
\end{proof}
