\begin{lemma}
Let $k>0$ be an integer and let $A$ be a nonnegative real $n\times n$ matrix
whose row and column sums are $k$. Then $B_{ij}=A_{ij}/k$ is nonnegative,
every row and column sum of $B$ is one, and
$\operatorname{per}(A)=k^n\operatorname{per}(B)$. This includes $n=0$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Division by the positive real number $k$ preserves nonnegativity.
Finite sums commute with division, so every row and column sum becomes
$k/k=1$. Entrywise $A=kB$. For each permutation in Mathlib's permanent
there are exactly $n$ factors; factoring $k$ from each gives
$\prod_j A_{\sigma(j),j}=k^n\prod_jB_{\sigma(j),j}$.
Summing proves the identity, also for the empty product.
\end{proof}
