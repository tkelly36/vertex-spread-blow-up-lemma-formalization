\subsection*{Author's correction: the colour-round induction}

The following argument replaces the former minimum-exposure ordering and the
exposure-indexed error hierarchy in the proof of
\cref{lem:spread-blowup-auxiliary}.  The statement of the lemma is unchanged.
The correction uses a bounded number of rounds obtained by colouring the
square of the reduced graph.  This colouring is unrelated to the equitable
colouring of $H^2[X_i]$ used earlier to refine the source clusters.

We first record the two standard robust forms of the regularity inputs that
we use.  They are stated for parts differing by at most one because this is
the only unequal-size case needed below.

\begin{lemma}[Quantitative spanning thinning]\label{lem:addendum-near-equal-thinning}
Let $0<\rho<D\leq1$ and $0<\gamma<\rho$.  There are $\beta_0>0$ and
$n_*$ such that the following holds.  Let $L,R$ be disjoint sets, put
$s\coloneqq\min\{|L|,|R|\}$, and suppose that $s\geq n_*$ and
$\lvert |L|-|R|\rvert\leq1$.  If $F[L,R]$ is
$(\beta,D)$-super-regular for some $0<\beta\leq\beta_0$, then it contains
a spanning $(\gamma,\rho)$-super-regular subgraph.  All degree conditions
are normalised by the actual opposite-part size.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Put $q\coloneqq\rho/D$.  Choose $\xi>0$ and then
$0<\beta_0\leq\gamma$ so that $q\beta_0+\xi\leq\gamma$.  Retain each edge
independently with probability $q$.  For every vertex, a Chernoff bound
shows that its retained degree differs from $q$ times its old degree by at
most $\xi$ times the opposite-part size, except with probability
$\exp(-\Omega(s))$.  If $U\subseteq L$ and $W\subseteq R$ are above the
$\gamma$-thresholds, the retained edge count differs from
$q e_F(U,W)$ by at most $\xi|U||W|$, except with probability
$\exp(-\Omega(|U||W|))=\exp(-\Omega(s^2))$.  There are only
$2^{|L|+|R|}=\exp(O(s))$ choices of $(U,W)$.  A union bound over all
vertices and all such pairs is therefore smaller than one when $s$ is
large.  Fix an outcome outside this union.  Since $\beta\leq\gamma$, old
regularity applies to every tested $(U,W)$, and
\[
  d_{F'}(U,W)=q\,d_F(U,W)\pm\xi
             =\rho\pm(q\beta+\xi).
\]
The same calculation for every degree proves the result.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Near-equal Four Graphs lemma]\label{lem:addendum-near-equal-four-graphs}
Fix $0<d_{12},d_{13},d_{23}\leq1$, and put
$q\coloneqq d_{13}d_{23}$.  For every sufficiently small
$0<\eps'<\min\{q,1\}$ there are $\eps_0>0$, $0<a\leq1$, and $m_0$ such that the
following holds.  Let $m\geq m_0$, let
\[
  |W_1|=|W_2|=m,
  \qquad |W_3|=k\in\{m-1,m,m+1\},
\]
and suppose that $F_{12},F_{13},F_{23}$ are respectively
$(\eps,d_{12})$-, $(\eps,d_{13})$-, and
$(\eps,d_{23})$-super-regular on the corresponding pairs, where
$0<\eps\leq\eps_0$.  Degrees are normalised by the actual opposite-part
size.  Suppose also that every $vu\in E(F_{12})$, with $v\in W_1$ and
$u\in W_2$, satisfies
\[
 \bigl|N_{F_{13}}(v)\cap N_{F_{23}}(u)\cap W_3\bigr|
      =(q\pm\eps)k.                                  \tag{B1}
\]
Then $F_{12}$ has a perfect matching.  If $\sigma:W_1\to W_2$ is chosen
uniformly from its perfect matchings, let $A_\sigma$ be the graph on
$(W_1,W_3)$ in which
\[
 vw\in E(A_\sigma)
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 vw\in E(F_{13})\ \text{and}\ \sigma(v)w\in E(F_{23}).
\]
Then
\[
 \Pr\bigl[A_\sigma\text{ is }(\eps',q)\text{-super-regular}\bigr]
      \geq1-\bigl(1-a\bigr)^{m}.                     \tag{B2}
\]
The constants are uniform over the three permitted values of $k$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
We indicate why the usual proof of the Four Graphs lemma is uniform in this
range of sizes.  Its matching set-image estimate says that, for fixed
$d_{12},\lambda,\delta>0$, there are $c<1$, an admissible input-error bound,
and a size threshold such that, whenever $S\subseteq W_1$ and
$T\subseteq W_2$ have size at least $\lambda m$, a uniformly random perfect
matching $\sigma$ of $F_{12}$ satisfies
\[
 \Pr\left[
  \left|\,|\sigma(S)\cap T|-\frac{|S||T|}{m}\right|>\delta m
 \right]\leq c^m.                                    \tag{B3}
\]
This is the standard two-edge-switching estimate used in the proof of the
Four Graphs lemma~\cite{RR99}; its constants do not depend on $S$ or $T$.
Choose $0<\delta\ll\eps'\ll q,d_{12},d_{13},d_{23}$ and then choose
$\eps_0$ below the finitely many input bounds in (B3), using lower set-size
bounds smaller than
\[
 d_{13}/2,\quad d_{23}/2,\quad
 (d_{13}/2)^{2},\quad (d_{23}/2)^{2}.
\]
For these choices, super-regularity of $F_{12}$ also guarantees a perfect
matching.

For $v\in W_1$, (B1) applied to $v\sigma(v)$ gives deterministically
\[
  \deg_{A_\sigma}(v)=(q\pm\eps)k.                    \tag{B4}
\]
For $w\in W_3$, set $S_w=N_{F_{13}}(w)\cap W_1$ and
$T_w=N_{F_{23}}(w)\cap W_2$.  These sets have sizes
$(d_{13}\pm\eps)m$ and $(d_{23}\pm\eps)m$, while
$\deg_{A_\sigma}(w)=|\sigma(S_w)\cap T_w|$.  Thus (B3), followed by a
union bound over $k\leq m+1$ vertices, gives
\[
  \deg_{A_\sigma}(w)=(q\pm O(\eps+\delta))m          \tag{B5}
\]
simultaneously for all $w$, except with probability at most $(m+1)c^m$.

The standard good-codegree consequence of regularity says that all but
$O(\eps)k^2$ ordered pairs $(w,w')\in W_3^2$ have their two common
neighbourhoods in $W_1$ and $W_2$ of sizes
$(d_{13}^2\pm O(\eps))m$ and $(d_{23}^2\pm O(\eps))m$.  Apply (B3) to
these two sets and take a union bound over at most $(m+1)^{2}$ pairs.  Except
with additional probability at most $(m+1)^{2}c^{m}$, every such pair has
\[
 |N_{A_\sigma}(w)\cap N_{A_\sigma}(w')|
       =(q^2\pm O(\eps+\delta))m.                    \tag{B6}
\]
Choose $\delta$ and then $\eps_0$ sufficiently small in terms of $\eps'$.
The degree--codegree criterion, applied with the actual part sizes, turns
equations (B4)--(B6) into $(\eps',q)$-super-regularity.  Finally,
\[
  \bigl((m+1)+(m+1)^{2}\bigr)c^{m}\leq\bigl(1-a\bigr)^{m}
\]
for some $a>0$ and every sufficiently large $m$.  The proof used only
$m-1\leq k\leq m+1$, which proves the asserted uniformity.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Corrected proof of \cref{lem:spread-blowup-auxiliary}]
Put $Q\coloneqq \Delta^2+1$.  Let $R^{\langle 2\rangle}$ be the graph on
$V(R)$ in which distinct vertices are adjacent if they are adjacent in $R$ or
have a common neighbour in $R$.  Since
\[
    \Delta(R^{\langle 2\rangle})
        \leq \Delta+\Delta(\Delta-1)=\Delta^2,
\]
there is a proper colouring
\[
    c:V(R)\longrightarrow\{0,\dots,Q-1\}.
\]
We allow colours which are not used, and write
$B_t\coloneqq c^{-1}(t)$.  Thus two clusters in the same $B_t$ are
nonadjacent in $R$ and have disjoint neighbourhoods in $R$.  In particular,
each cluster outside $B_t$ has at most one neighbour in $B_t$.

We first fix the density parameters recursively.  Let $p_0=d$.  For
$0\leq x<\Delta$, having defined $p_x$, choose $0<\rho_x<p_x$ and put
\[
    p_{x+1}\coloneqq \rho_x d/2.
\]
Finally choose $0<\rho_\Delta<p_\Delta$.  For example, one may take
$\rho_x=p_x/2$ at every step.  The index $x$ records the number
of already embedded neighbours of a cluster.  Our invariant stores a
spanning super-regular graph of density $p_x$ contained in the true updated
candidacy graph; it does not assert that the whole updated candidacy graph
has exactly that density.

Next choose error parameters
\[
    0<\eta_0\ll\eta_1\ll\cdots\ll\eta_Q
        \ll d,1/\Delta
\]
from right to left.  The choice is finite and depends only on $d$ and
$\Delta$.  Here is the precise order of choice.  After $\eta_{t+1}$ has
been chosen, choose $\omega_{t+1}>0$ below $\eta_{t+1}$ and below the
minimum of the finitely many admissible output-error caps in
\cref{lem:addendum-near-equal-four-graphs} for the density triples used
below.  For every ordered pair
\[
    (x,y)\in\{0,\dots,\Delta\}\times\{0,\dots,\Delta-1\},
\]
the near-equal Four Graphs lemma, with output error $\omega_{t+1}$ and
density triple $(\rho_x,\rho_y,d/2)$, supplies a positive admissible input
cutoff.  Choose one common $\theta_t>0$ below all of these cutoffs and also
below the perfect-matching existence and uniform-spread cutoffs for the
finitely many densities $\rho_x$.  Instantiate Four Graphs at input
$\theta_t$, and denote its exponential constant for $(x,y)$ by
$a_{t,x,y}$.  Likewise, let $M_{t,x}$ be a constant for the
uniform-perfect-matching spread bound at density $\rho_x$.  Thus
\[
    0<a_{t,x,y}\leq1
    \quad\text{and}\quad
    M_{t,x}\geq1.
\]
Only after these choices do we choose a post-thinning and padding error
$\zeta_t>0$ so small that
\[
    2\sqrt{\zeta_t}\leq\theta_t,\qquad
    \zeta_t+\Delta\sqrt{\zeta_t}\leq\theta_t,
    \qquad \zeta_t+2\Delta\zeta_t\leq\theta_t,       \tag{A1}
\]
and so that
\[
  2\zeta_t<\min\left\{\min_{0\leq x\leq\Delta}\rho_x,
       \frac d2,\min_{0\leq x\leq\Delta}(1-\rho_x),1-\frac d2\right\}.
                                                               \tag{A2}
\]
Choose an inner reserve $0<\widehat\zeta_t<\zeta_t$.  Finally choose
$\eta_t$ sufficiently small that every required spanning thinning of an
$(\eta_t,p_x)$-super-regular witness has error at most
$\widehat\zeta_t$, and also so that
\[
  2\eta_t<\min_{0\leq y<\Delta}(p_y-p_{y+1}).
\]
It is important here to use all relevant ordered pairs
$(x,y)$: the colour-round order gives no inequality between the exposure of
the current cluster and that of a future cluster.  Taking the minimum of the
finitely many $a_{t,x,y}$ and the maximum of the finitely many $M_{t,x}$
gives constants $0<a\leq1$ and $M\geq1$ which depend only on $d$ and
$\Delta$.  We take $C\geq2M$.  After $r$ is fixed, choose $n_0$ last.
Besides all thinning, row-padding, perfect-matching existence,
uniform-spread, and Four Graphs thresholds, it is large enough that, for
every $t<Q$ and $n\geq n_0$,
\[
    \Delta\bigl(1-a\bigr)^{n}\leq\tfrac12,\qquad C/n\leq1. \tag{A3}
\]
and
\[
    \omega_{t+1}+2/n\leq\eta_{t+1},
    \qquad \omega_{t+1}+1/(\eta_{t+1}n)\leq\eta_{t+1}. \tag{A4}
\]
The row insertion and integer-rounding errors are required to be at most
$\zeta_t-\widehat\zeta_t$.  Finally choose the input regularity parameter in
\cref{lem:spread-blowup-auxiliary} at most $\eta_0$, with $\eta_0$ also
below every host-pair thinning cutoff from density $d$ to $d/2$ in all
$Q$ rounds.

We embed the colour classes $B_0,\dots,B_{Q-1}$ in this order and process the
clusters inside one colour class sequentially.  For
$S\subseteq B_t$, let
\[
    P_{t,S}
        \coloneqq \bigcup_{s<t}B_s\cup S
\]
be the set of clusters already processed.  If $f$ embeds the corresponding
source clusters and $j\notin P_{t,S}$, write $A_f^j$ for the spanning
subgraph of $A^j$ in which $xv$ is an edge exactly when it was an edge of
$A^j$ and $f(u)v\in E(G)$ for every already embedded neighbour $u$ of $x$.
The within-round invariant is the following.  There is a
$(C/n)$-vertex-spread distribution on compatible
partial embeddings $f$ of the source clusters indexed by $P_{t,S}$ such that,
for every unembedded cluster $j$, if
\[
    x_j\coloneqq |N_R(j)\cap P_{t,S}|,
\]
then the true updated candidacy graph $A_f^j$ contains a spanning witness of
density $p_{x_j}$ which is
\[
    \begin{cases}
      (\eta_{t+1},p_{x_j})\text{-super-regular},
        &\text{if }N_R(j)\cap S\neq\varnothing,\\
      (\eta_t,p_{x_j})\text{-super-regular},
        &\text{if }N_R(j)\cap S=\varnothing.
    \end{cases}                                      \tag{*}
\]
The first intersection in $(*)$ has size at most one.  This touched/untouched
distinction is essential: a witness changed earlier in round $t$ is only
required to have error $\eta_{t+1}$.

The invariant holds at the start with $t=0$ and $S=\varnothing$, using the
original candidacy graphs.  Suppose it holds for $S\subseteq B_t$, and choose
$i\in B_t\setminus S$.  Since $B_t$ is independent in
$R^{\langle 2\rangle}$, we have
\[
    N_R(i)\cap S=\varnothing
    \quad\text{and}\quad
    N_R(j)\cap S=\varnothing
        \quad\text{for every }j\in N_R(i)\setminus P_{t,S}.
\]
Consequently, the witness for the current cluster $i$ and every future
witness which is tested while embedding $i$ all have the common input error
$\eta_t$, although their densities and exposure indices may differ.

Fix an outcome of the partial embedding up to this point.  Apply
\cref{lem:addendum-near-equal-thinning} to thin the current witness of
exposure $x_i$ to density $\rho_{x_i}$, each relevant future witness of
exposure $x_j$ to density $\rho_{x_j}$, and each host pair $G[V_i,V_j]$ to
density $d/2$, initially with error at most $\widehat\zeta_t$.  Retain from
the thinned current witness
only those edges which satisfy the required degree-codegree test for every
unembedded $j\in N_R(i)$, with the window
\[
  \bigl(\rho_{x_j}d/2\pm2\zeta_t\bigr)|V_j|
\]
normalised by the actual future part.  We spell out the simultaneous
good-edge estimate,
since it is the point at which the common error $\eta_t$ is needed.  After
thinning, all pairs have error at most $\zeta_t$.  For one test and one fixed
endpoint of the current pair, regularity of the two tested pairs shows that
at most $2\zeta_t$ times the opposite part size fail the codegree test.  Thus
at most $2\Delta\zeta_t$ of the degree can be lost.  If $U$ and $W$ are
subsets of the two current parts of relative size greater than $\theta_t$,
the standard bad-pair count shows that at most a
$\sqrt{\zeta_t}$ proportion of $U\times W$ fails any one test.  Hence the
total density loss on $U\times W$ is at most
$\Delta\sqrt{\zeta_t}$.  The three inequalities in (A1), together with
regularity after a bounded density loss, show that the retained current pair
is $(\theta_t,\rho_{x_i})$-super-regular.  They also show that it and each
thinned future witness and host pair form the appropriate near-equal regular
triplet.  This proves the full super-regular conclusion needed here, rather
than only the edgewise codegree condition.

By the perfect-matching existence threshold built into the hierarchy, the
retained current pair has a perfect matching.  Choose one uniformly.  Its
law is $(M/|X_i|)$-spread and therefore $(M/n)$-spread.  For each
unembedded $j\in N_R(i)$, the Four Graphs conclusion fails with probability
at most $\bigl(1-a\bigr)^{|X_i|}\leq\bigl(1-a\bigr)^{n}$.  By (A3), a union
bound shows that all
of the at most $\Delta$ local conclusions hold simultaneously with
probability at least $1/2$.  We condition this one matching on that local
success event.  The unconditioned
matching law is $(M/n)$-spread, so the conditioned law is $(2M/n)$-spread and
hence $(C/n)$-spread.  For each such $j$ we have $x_j<\Delta$, because $i$
is an unembedded neighbour of $j$.  Transport the Four Graphs output
along the saturating source matching to obtain, inside the true updated
candidacy graph, an $(\eta_{t+1},p_{x_j+1})$-super-regular witness.  Every
other unembedded witness is unchanged.  This proves the invariant with
$S\cup\{i\}$ in place of $S$.

The conditioning in the preceding paragraph is local to the current cluster.
We do not condition on one global event for all of $B_t$, whose probability
could depend on $|B_t|$ and hence on $r$.  Instead, the clusters of $B_t$ are
processed sequentially.  At every step, conditional on the entire past, the
new matching law is $(C/n)$-spread.  The chain rule therefore preserves the
joint $(C/n)$-vertex-spread bound.  At the end of round $t$, every witness
touched in that round already has error $\eta_{t+1}$, while every untouched
witness has the stronger error $\eta_t$ and may be viewed as having error
$\eta_{t+1}$.  This is precisely the invariant at the start of round $t+1$.

For completeness, we record the near-equal transport used above.  Write
$m=|X_i|=|V_i|$ and $k=|X_j|=|V_j|$, so
$m,k\in\{n,n+1\}$ and $|m-k|\leq1$.  The near-equal Four Graphs statement is
normalised using the actual part sizes.  If $m=k$, the output is transported
directly along the perfect source matching.  If $m=k+1$, the source matching
leaves one current source vertex unmatched.  Adjoin one artificial row to the
pulled-back $F_{13}$ pair: choose any subset of $V_j$ whose size is in the
required $(\rho_{x_j}\pm\zeta_t)k$ window.  Adjoining this single row
preserves super-regularity with the reserved slack.  Regularity of the
thinned host pair shows that all but at most $2\zeta_t m$ possible partners
of this artificial row pass this future-neighbour test, so the simultaneous
good-edge calculation above still applies to the entire current pair.  We
apply Four Graphs and then delete the matching-induced row corresponding to
the unmatched current vertex.

If $k=m+1$, the saturating matching leaves one future source vertex $z$
unmatched.  Since $H[X_i,X_j]$ is a matching, $z$ has no neighbour in $X_i$;
its row in the true candidacy graph is therefore unchanged when $X_i$ is
embedded.  Apply Four Graphs to the pullback along the saturating matching
and transport its output first to $X_j\setminus\{z\}$.  The stored old
witness already supplies the row of $z$, of degree at least
$(p_{x_j}-\eta_t)|V_j|$.  Our parameter and size choices make this larger
than $p_{x_j+1}|V_j|+1$, since
$p_{x_j+1}=\rho_{x_j}d/2<p_{x_j}$.  Choose a subset of that row's
neighbourhood whose size is the nearest integer to
$p_{x_j+1}|V_j|$, and adjoin precisely this row to the Four Graphs output.
The result remains a subgraph of the true updated candidacy graph.

In either unequal case, the row-stability calculation is explicit: deleting
or adjoining one row to an $(\omega_{t+1},p_{x_j+1})$-super-regular pair
preserves the degree conditions provided
$\omega_{t+1}+2/n\leq\eta_{t+1}$, and preserves regularity provided
$\omega_{t+1}+1/(\eta_{t+1}n)\leq\eta_{t+1}$.  These are exactly (A4).
The probabilistic estimates remain uniform because
\[
    M/m\leq M/n
    \quad\text{and}\quad
    \bigl(1-a\bigr)^{m}\leq\bigl(1-a\bigr)^{n}.
\]
This proves the required one-row stability in both unequal-size cases and
keeps every transported witness inside the true candidacy graph.

After the last colour round every cluster has been embedded.  Repeated use of
the chain rule gives a $(C/n)$-vertex-spread distribution on full compatible
embeddings of $H$ into $G$.  This proves
\cref{lem:spread-blowup-auxiliary} with the same statement and quantifier
order as before.  No minimum-exposure ordering of the clusters is used.
\end{proof}
